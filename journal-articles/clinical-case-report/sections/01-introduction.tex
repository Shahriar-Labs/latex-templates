\section{Introduction}

Acute aortic dissection is one of the most catastrophic vascular emergencies encountered in
clinical practice, with an estimated incidence of 3--5 per 100{,}000 persons per year
\cite{erbel2014}. The hallmark presentation—sudden, severe, tearing or ripping chest pain
radiating to the back—is absent in up to 17\% of cases, making diagnosis challenging
\cite{meszaros2000}.

Risk factors include hypertension (present in 72\% of cases), Marfan syndrome, bicuspid aortic
valve, and cocaine use \cite{nienaber2016}. When the diagnosis is missed in the emergency
setting, in-hospital mortality may exceed 50\% \cite{bossone2018}.

We report a case of DeBakey type I AAD in a young male presenting with interscapular pain
initially attributed to a musculoskeletal cause, highlighting diagnostic pitfalls and the
importance of early cross-sectional imaging.
