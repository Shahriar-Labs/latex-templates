% -------- Abstract --------
\begin{abstractbox}
\textbf{Background:}
Acute aortic dissection (AAD) is a life-threatening emergency that carries a mortality rate
exceeding 1\% per hour if untreated. Atypical presentations—particularly in younger patients
without classical risk factors—frequently delay diagnosis.

\smallskip
\textbf{Case Presentation:}
A 34-year-old male with poorly controlled hypertension presented with progressive interscapular
pain of 6-hour duration mischaracterised as musculoskeletal. Serial aortic computed tomography
angiography (CTA) confirmed a type A dissection extending from the aortic root to the
iliac bifurcation (DeBakey type I). Emergency open surgical repair with composite graft
replacement of the ascending aorta was performed. The patient was discharged neurologically
intact on day 12.

\smallskip
\textbf{Conclusions:}
This case underscores the importance of maintaining a high index of suspicion for AAD in young
hypertensive patients presenting with back or chest pain, even when the presentation is
atypical. Prompt CTA and multidisciplinary involvement are essential for favourable outcomes.

\smallskip
\textbf{Keywords:}
aortic dissection; hypertension; chest pain; computed tomography angiography; cardiac surgery
\end{abstractbox}
