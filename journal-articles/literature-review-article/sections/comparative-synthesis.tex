data extraction (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}): consistency model, staleness bound, latency overhead
relative to a no-consistency baseline, fault-tolerance mechanism, and the trade-off each system makes
explicit in its own evaluation.

Three observations follow directly from the table. First, the latency premium for strong consistency
has fallen from 1.8$\times$ (Nexa, 2015) to 1.3--1.4$\times$ (Apex/Nimbus, 2021--2022) as sequencer and
lease designs matured --- the cost of correctness is not fixed, it is an engineering variable that the
field has been actively driving down. Second, every placement-aware system (Nimbus, Meridian) reports a
\emph{lower} overhead than its non-placement-aware counterpart in the same cluster, supporting the
cluster D claim that placement and consistency should not be optimised separately. Third, no system in
the corpus reports an energy or backhaul-bandwidth cost column, which Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
identifies as a synthesis gap.

\begin{keytakeaway}
Placement-aware variants of both strong (Nimbus) and tunable (Meridian) protocols report lower latency
overhead than their placement-agnostic counterparts, which is the strongest quantitative evidence in
the corpus that consistency and placement should be co-designed.
\end{keytakeaway}
