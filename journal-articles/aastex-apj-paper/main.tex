\begin{abstract}
We present high-precision transit photometry of TOI-4521\,b, a warm
sub-Neptune ($R_p = 2.71 \pm 0.09\,R_\oplus$, $T_\mathrm{eq} \approx
680\,\mathrm{K}$) orbiting a solar-type star with an orbital period of
$P = 14.37\,\mathrm{days}$.  Using 47 transit events observed with the
Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (\textit{TESS}) across Sectors 14,
40, and 67, supplemented by two ground-based light curves from the
Las Cumbres Observatory Global Telescope (LCOGT) network, we refine the
planetary radius to $R_p = 2.71 \pm 0.09\,R_\oplus$ and the orbital
inclination to $i = 88.63 \pm 0.15\,\mathrm{deg}$.  A joint
Mandel--Agol transit model simultaneously fit to all datasets yields a
transit-depth precision of $\sigma_\delta = 35\,\mathrm{ppm}$, enabling
meaningful Transmission Spectroscopy Metric (TSM) constraints.  We
compute $\mathrm{TSM} = 94 \pm 12$, placing TOI-4521\,b among the most
favorable targets for atmospheric characterization with the
\textit{James Webb Space Telescope} (\textit{JWST}) in the sub-Neptune
regime.  Atmospheric retrieval simulations predict that a $20\,\mathrm{hr}$
NIRSpec/G395H program would distinguish a hydrogen-dominated from a
high-mean-molecular-weight atmosphere at $>5\sigma$ confidence.
Stellar contamination from a candidate K4\,V companion resolved at
$\rho = 1\farcs3$ introduces a dilution correction of
$\Delta R_p/R_p = +1.4\%$, which we propagate fully into our reported
uncertainties.
\end{abstract}
