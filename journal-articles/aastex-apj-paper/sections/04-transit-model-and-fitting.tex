\section{Transit Model and Fitting}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Mandel--Agol Light-Curve Model}
\label{sec:ma}

We modeled each transit event with the analytic \citet{MandelAgol2002}
quadratic limb-darkening model as implemented in \texttt{batman}
\citep{Kreidberg2015}.  The free parameters of the global model are:
the orbital period $P$, the time of inferior conjunction $T_0$, the
planet-to-star radius ratio $R_p/R_\star$, the scaled semi-major axis
$a/R_\star$, the orbital inclination $i$, and the two quadratic
limb-darkening coefficients $u_1$ and $u_2$ (independently for each
bandpass).

The transit duration for a circular orbit is
\begin{equation}
   T_{14} = \frac{P}{\pi}
            \arcsin\!\left[
              \frac{R_\star}{a}
              \sqrt{\!\left(1 + \frac{R_p}{R_\star}\right)^{\!2}
                    - \left(\frac{a}{R_\star}\cos i\right)^{\!2}}
            \right] ,
   \label{eq:t14}
\end{equation}
which, for our best-fit parameters, gives $T_{14} = 2.91 \pm 0.04\,\mathrm{hr}$.

\subsection{Bayesian Parameter Estimation}
\label{sec:bayes}

Posterior distributions were sampled with the affine-invariant ensemble
MCMC sampler \texttt{emcee} \citep{ForemanMackey2013} using 200 walkers
and $5\times 10^4$ steps per walker after a burn-in of $10^4$ steps.
Convergence was assessed via the Gelman--Rubin statistic ($\hat{R}<1.01$
for all parameters) and integrated autocorrelation times.
