\section{Methodology}
The SDM model consists of a network of $N$ leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons connected through dynamic synaptic junctions.
The membrane potential $V_i(t)$ of neuron $i$ is governed by the following differential equation:
\begin{equation}
\tau_m \frac{dV_i(t)}{dt} = -(V_i(t) - V_{rest}) + R \left( I_{ext,i}(t) + I_{syn,i}(t) \right)
\label{eq:membrane}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_m$ represents the membrane time constant, $V_{rest}$ is the resting potential, $R$ is the membrane resistance, $I_{ext,i}(t)$ is the external stimulus, and $I_{syn,i}(t)$ is the total synaptic current received by neuron $i$.

The synaptic current $I_{syn,i}(t)$ is computed as the sum of all presynaptic inputs scaled by the dynamic synaptic weights $W_{ij}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
I_{syn,i}(t) = \sum_{j \neq i} W_{ij}(t) \sum_{k} \alpha(t - t_{j,k})
\label{eq:synaptic_current}
\end{equation}
where $t_{j,k}$ is the time of the $k$-th spike emitted by presynaptic neuron $j$, and $\alpha(t)$ is the synaptic kernel function defined as:
\begin{equation}
\alpha(t) = \frac{t}{\tau_s} \exp\left(1 - \frac{t}{\tau_s}\right) \Theta(t)
\end{equation}
with $\tau_s$ representing the synaptic decay time constant and $\Theta(t)$ being the Heaviside step function.

\subsection{Synaptic Dynamics and Weight Adaptation}
The synaptic weights $W_{ij}(t)$ are adapted based on a modified spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) rule combined with a homeostatic scaling mechanism.
The STDP weight update is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dW_{ij}(t)}{dt} = \eta \left( A_+ \exp\left( - \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_+} \right) - A_- \exp\left( - \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_-} \right) \right) - \gamma (W_{ij}(t) - W_0)
\label{eq:plasticity}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t = t_{post} - t_{pre}$ is the time difference between post- and presynaptic spikes, $\eta$ is the learning rate, $\tau_+$ and $\tau_-$ are the temporal windows for potentiation and depression respectively, and $\gamma$ is the homeostatic scaling factor that pulls the weight back to its baseline value $W_0$.

\subsection{Network Architecture and Topology}
We arrange the neurons in a hierarchical structure consisting of three distinct layers: Vertex (input), Meridian (processing), and Apex (output).
The connections within the Meridian layer are recurrent and sparse, mimicking the dense local connectivity of cortical columns.
Fig~\ref{fig:network} illustrates the connectivity layout.
