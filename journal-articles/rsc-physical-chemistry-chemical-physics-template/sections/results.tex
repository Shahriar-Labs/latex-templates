\section{Results}
\subsection{Steady-State Characterization}
The steady-state absorption and emission spectra of Nexa-101 and Nexa-102 in both thin films and dilute solutions are shown in the Supplementary Information. Thin-film absorption spectra of both compounds display a broad absorption band in the visible range, with absorption maxima ($\lambda_{\text{max}}$) located at 540\,nm for Nexa-101 and 555\,nm for Nexa-102. The red-shift in Nexa-102 is attributed to the increased conjugation length of the terminal acceptor units.

The photophysical and thermodynamic properties derived from steady-state measurements are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:properties}. The optical bandgap ($E_{\text{g}}$) was estimated from the absorption onset, yielding values of 1.84\,eV and 1.76\,eV for Nexa-101 and Nexa-102, respectively.

\subsection{Excited-State Dynamics and Transient Absorption}
To resolve the ultrafast photophysical pathways, we performed femtosecond TA spectroscopy on the thin films. Figure~\ref{fig:kinetics} shows the transient absorption decay kinetics at 650\,nm for both Nexa-101 (Sample A) and a reference Synapse-202 derivative (Sample B).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}
    \begin{axis}[
      width=0.45\textwidth,
      height=5cm,
      xlabel={Delay Time (ps)},
      ylabel={Differential Absorption ($\Delta A \times 10^3$)},
      xmin=-1, xmax=10,
      ymin=-2, ymax=10,
      axis lines=left,
      grid=both,
      grid style={dashed, gray!30},
      legend pos=north east,
      title={\sffamily Transient Absorption Decay}
    ]
      \addplot[color=RSCBurgundy, thick, domain=0:10, samples=100] {8*exp(-x/1.5) + 1.5*exp(-x/8)};
      \addlegendentry{Sample A}
      \addplot[color=RSCDarkTeal, thick, dashed, domain=0:10, samples=100] {9*exp(-x/0.8) + 0.5*exp(-x/5)};
      \addlegendentry{Sample B}
    \end{axis}
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \caption{Transient absorption decay kinetics at 650\,nm for Sample A (Nexa compound) and Sample B (Synapse derivative) showing biexponential relaxation pathways.}
  \label{fig:kinetics}
\end{figure}

The kinetics were fitted using a biexponential decay function:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A(t) = A_1 e^{-t/\tau_1} + A_2 e^{-t/\tau_2}
\end{equation}
For Nexa-101, the fast decay component ($\tau_1 = 1.5$\,ps) is assigned to singlet exciton relaxation and energy transfer, while the longer component ($\tau_2 = 8.0$\,ps) represents triplet formation via intersystem crossing (ISC). In contrast, the reference compound Sample B exhibits a much faster primary decay ($\tau_1 = 0.8$\,ps), which we attribute to rapid charge separation at the donor-acceptor interface.
