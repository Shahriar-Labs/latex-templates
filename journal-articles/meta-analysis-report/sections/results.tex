\section{Results}

\subsection{Study Selection and Characteristics}

The search returned 41 records, of which 6 independent corporate pilots met all eligibility
criteria and were retained for synthesis \citep{chandrasekaran2023nexa, lindqvist2022synapse,
ferreira2023vertex, okafor2024nimbus, rahman2022apex, vogel2023meridian}. Table~\ref{tab:studies}
summarizes their design characteristics. Across the six trials, 457 employees worked a
compressed four-day schedule and 434 continued on a standard five-day schedule, spanning
technology, analytics, manufacturing, cloud infrastructure, logistics, and healthcare
administration settings.

\subsection{Pooled Effect and Forest Plot}

The random-effects pooled estimate was $g = 0.33$ (95\% CI $[0.23, 0.43]$, $z = 6.40$,
$p < 0.001$), indicating a small-to-moderate advantage in standardized output for teams on
the compressed schedule. Figure~\ref{fig:forest} displays the trial-level and pooled
estimates; marker area is proportional to random-effects weight, and the gold diamond marks
the pooled effect and its confidence interval.

\subsection{Heterogeneity}

Between-trial heterogeneity was moderate. The panel below summarizes the diagnostic
statistics alongside the pooled effect and its prediction interval.

\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}{0.31\linewidth}
  \statbox{$I^2$ statistic}{36\%}{moderate heterogeneity}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}{0.31\linewidth}

\end{minipage}

\begin{minipage}{0.31\linewidth}

\begin{minipage}{0.31\linewidth}
  \statbox{Egger's test}{$p = 0.38$}{no small-study bias}
\end{minipage}
\par

The non-significant $Q$ statistic ($p = 0.166$) alongside a moderate $I^2$ of 36\% suggests
that some of the observed variation in effect size is attributable to genuine differences
across sectors rather than sampling error alone, though the number of trials is too small to
support formal subgroup or meta-regression analysis. The 95\% prediction interval
$[0.02, 0.64]$ remains entirely on the side favoring the compressed schedule, indicating
that a new trial in a comparable setting would be very likely to observe a positive, if
variable, effect.

\subsection{Publication Bias and Sensitivity}
\label{sec:pubbias}

Egger's regression test found no evidence of small-study effects ($p = 0.38$); the
corresponding funnel plot (Appendix~\ref{app:funnel}) shows a broadly symmetric scatter
around the pooled estimate. A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis confirmed that no single
trial was responsible for the overall result: removing any one study shifted the pooled
estimate by no more than 0.04 and left it statistically significant in every iteration.
