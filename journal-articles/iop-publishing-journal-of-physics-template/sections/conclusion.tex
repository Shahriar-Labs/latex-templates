\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have mapped the critical current response of $25$~nm NbN superconducting thin films to heavy-ion irradiation across a fluence range of $0$--$1\times10^{12}$~ions~cm$^{-2}$, combining resistive transport, field-dependent transport critical current, and magneto-optical flux imaging. The critical temperature falls monotonically with fluence, while the zero-field critical current density at $4.2$~K rises to a maximum enhancement of a factor of $1.8$ at an intermediate dose of $5\times10^{11}$~ions~cm$^{-2}$ before declining at higher fluence. Dew-Hughes pinning-force scaling analysis attributes this optimum to a crossover from weak, point-like pinning in the lightly irradiated films to strong, correlated pinning by surface-normal damage columns at intermediate dose, a picture corroborated by the suppression of dendritic flux avalanches observed by magneto-optical imaging at the same dose. Beyond this optimum, continued growth in pinning-site density is outweighed by the accompanying suppression of $T_c$ and the superfluid density, returning $J_c$ toward its pristine value even as the film accumulates further irradiation damage.

For device design, these results indicate that a fluence near $5\times10^{11}$~ions~cm$^{-2}$ offers the most favourable trade-off for $25$~nm NbN films operated near $4.2$~K, combining a substantial $J_c$ enhancement with a $T_c$ that remains comfortably above typical SNSPD and SRF operating temperatures. Future work should extend this mapping to a second ion species and energy to test the predicted shift of the optimal dose with track cross-section, and to complementary structural probes such as cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy to directly image the damage columns whose existence is here inferred from transport and magneto-optical signatures alone.
