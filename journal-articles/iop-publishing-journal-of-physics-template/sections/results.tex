\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Transition temperature and sample overview}
Table~\ref{tab:samples} summarises the four films studied. The critical temperature decreases monotonically with fluence, from $15.8$~K in the pristine film NbN-0 to $10.7$~K in the most heavily irradiated film NbN-3, a suppression of $5.1$~K over the fluence range studied. The transition width $\Delta T_c$ broadens in parallel, from $0.3$~K to $1.1$~K, consistent with irradiation-induced spatial inhomogeneity in the local order-parameter magnitude. The zero-field critical current density at $4.2$~K, by contrast, is non-monotonic in fluence: it rises by a factor of $1.8$ between NbN-0 and the intermediate-dose film NbN-2, then falls back toward the pristine value at the highest dose.

\subsection{Field-dependent critical current}
Figure~\ref{fig:jch} shows the transport critical current density as a function of perpendicular applied field at $4.2$~K for all four samples. Across the full field range measured ($0.1$--$9$~T), NbN-2 exhibits the highest $J_c$ at every field point, with the enhancement relative to the pristine film widening at intermediate fields ($1$--$4$~T) before narrowing again as all films converge toward the irreversibility field. NbN-3, despite receiving the highest ion dose and therefore the largest nominal density of pinning sites, falls below NbN-1 at fields above $2$~T, underscoring that pinning-site density alone does not determine $J_c$ once the accompanying suppression of the superfluid density becomes significant.

\subsection{Pinning force scaling}
Fitting the field-dependent pinning force density $F_p = J_c B$ to the Dew-Hughes form of equation~(\ref{eq:dewhughes}) yields exponent pairs of $(p,q) = (0.94\pm0.06,\,2.05\pm0.09)$ for NbN-1, $(0.51\pm0.04,\,1.98\pm0.07)$ for NbN-2, and $(0.44\pm0.05,\,1.91\pm0.10)$ for NbN-3. The pristine film NbN-0 is fitted for reference and gives $(1.12\pm0.08,\,2.11\pm0.12)$, close to the canonical weak point-pinning value $(1,2)$ expected for a film whose only disorder is intrinsic growth-related defect density. The exponent $p$ drops sharply between NbN-0/NbN-1 and NbN-2/NbN-3, consistent with a crossover from point-like to extended, correlated pinning as the ion-track density increases, while $q\approx2$ remains essentially unchanged across the series, indicating that the high-field falloff mechanism is not itself altered by irradiation.
