\section{Experimental methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Film growth}
NbN films were deposited by reactive DC magnetron sputtering from a $99.95\%$-purity niobium target in an Ar/N$_2$ atmosphere (partial pressure ratio $8:1$, total pressure $0.6$~Pa) onto $10\times10$~mm$^2$ c-plane sapphire substrates held at $800\,^{\circ}$C. Growth was monitored \textit{in situ} by reflection high-energy electron diffraction, and film thickness was fixed at $25\,\mathrm{nm}$ across the series, confirmed post-growth by X-ray reflectometry to within $\pm 0.6$~nm. All films were grown in the same deposition run to minimise batch-to-batch variation in stoichiometry, and a $3$~nm amorphous AlN capping layer was applied \textit{in situ} to prevent surface oxidation prior to patterning.

\subsection{Ion irradiation}
Irradiation was carried out at the $15$~MV tandem Van de Graaff accelerator at Apex National Laboratory using $220$~MeV Au$^{7+}$ ions incident at normal incidence. At this energy the projected ion range in NbN, estimated from stopping-and-range simulations, exceeds $9\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, more than two orders of magnitude greater than the film thickness; the ions therefore traverse the full film and deposit energy via electronic stopping at an approximately constant rate through its depth, producing continuous, surface-normal damage columns rather than buried defect clusters. Four fluences were used: $\Phi = 0$ (pristine reference, sample NbN-0), $1\times10^{11}$ (NbN-1), $5\times10^{11}$ (NbN-2), and $1\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{ions\,cm^{-2}}$ (NbN-3). The beam was rastered over each $10\times10$~mm$^2$ sample to achieve fluence uniformity better than $5\%$, monitored by a calibrated Faraday cup, and samples were held below $30\,^{\circ}$C throughout irradiation using a water-cooled stage to suppress thermally activated defect annealing.

\subsection{Transport characterisation}
Each irradiated film was patterned by photolithography and reactive-ion etching into a four-terminal Hall-bar geometry with a $100\,\mu\mathrm{m}$-wide current channel. Temperature-dependent resistance was measured in a Physical Property Measurement System at Meridian University from $300$~K down to $2$~K using a low-excitation AC four-probe technique ($10\,\mu\mathrm{A}$ RMS, $17.7$~Hz) to avoid self-heating. The critical temperature of each sample was defined as the midpoint of the resistive transition, and the transition width $\Delta T_c$ as the $10$--$90\%$ interval of the normal-state resistance drop.

Field-dependent critical currents were measured at $4.2$~K in a $^{3}$He cryostat equipped with a $9$~T superconducting magnet at the Cryogenic Materials Group, Synapse Research Park, with the applied field oriented perpendicular to the film plane. $I$--$V$ characteristics were recorded at each field value and $I_c$ extracted using a standard $1\,\mu\mathrm{V\,cm^{-1}}$ electric-field criterion. Field sweeps were performed after zero-field cooling, and each reported $I_c(H)$ point is the average of three consecutive sweeps to suppress the influence of occasional flux-jump events.

\subsection{Magneto-optical imaging}
Direct visualisation of the vortex penetration pattern was performed at the magneto-optical imaging laboratory of the Vertex Institute of Technology, using a Bi-doped yttrium iron garnet indicator film placed in direct contact with the sample surface and imaged in the Faraday geometry under a polarising microscope. Images were acquired during slow field ramps ($0.5$~mT~s$^{-1}$) at $4.2$~K to capture the spatial character of flux entry -- in particular, the presence or absence of dendritic flux avalanches, which are a well-established signature of a thermomagnetically unstable, weakly pinned film.
