\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Metallicity gradients}

The sample-median metallicity gradient is
$\langle\nabla\fe\rangle = -0.18 \pm 0.04\,\mathrm{dex}\,\re^{-1}$,
confirming that ETGs are more metal-rich at their centres.
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:gradients}, $\nabla\fe$ steepens with
stellar mass: galaxies above $\log(M_{\star}/\Msun) = 10.8$ exhibit
gradients $\approx 0.07\,\mathrm{dex}$ steeper than lower-mass
counterparts (Table~\ref{tab:sample}).
This trend is consistent with the expectation that more massive
systems experienced a deeper potential well during dissipative
collapse, driving stronger chemical stratification.

\subsection{Age gradients}

Age gradients are mild but statistically significant:
$\langle\nabla\logage\rangle = +0.06 \pm 0.03\,\mathrm{dex}\,\re^{-1}$.
The positive sign indicates that the centres are on average
$\sim$1\,Gyr \emph{older} than the outskirts at $2\,\re$,
a signature of inside-out quenching.
The trend strengthens above the same mass threshold of
$\log(M_{\star}/\Msun) = 10.8$ (Figure~\ref{fig:gradients}),
mirroring the metallicity behaviour.

\subsection{$\alpha$-element gradients}

In contrast to metallicity and age, the $\afe$ profiles are
consistent with zero gradient:
$\langle\nabla\afe\rangle = +0.01 \pm 0.02\,\mathrm{dex}\,\re^{-1}$.
Flat $\afe$ profiles imply that the ratio of core-collapse to
Type~Ia supernova enrichment did not vary significantly with radius,
i.e.\ that the star-formation time-scale was uniformly short
($\lesssim 1\,\mathrm{Gyr}$) across the galaxy body.
