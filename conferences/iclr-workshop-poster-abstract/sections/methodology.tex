\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Adaptive Sparsity Masks}

Let \(\mathbf{V} \in \R^{N_v \times d}\) and
\(\mathbf{L} \in \R^{N_l \times d}\) denote the vision and language
token sequences produced by their respective encoders, where
\(N_v, N_l\) are token counts and \(d\) is the hidden dimension.
At each cross-modal attention layer \(\ell\), \modelname{} computes a
soft retention score for every token:
\begin{equation}
  s^{(\ell)}_i = \sigma\!\left(\mathbf{w}^{(\ell)\top} \mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_i + b^{(\ell)}\right),
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_i\) is the hidden state of the \(i\)-th
token at layer \(\ell\), and \(\mathbf{w}^{(\ell)}, b^{(\ell)}\) are
learnable parameters. A differentiable top-\(k\) relaxation
\citep{nguyen2024sparse} converts scores into a binary-like mask
\(\mathbf{m}^{(\ell)} \in \{0,1\}^{N}\) during training; at inference,
exact top-\(k\) selection is used.

\subsection{Training Objective}

The overall loss is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}} +
    \lambda \sum_{\ell} \left\| \mathbf{m}^{(\ell)} \right\|_1,
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}\) is the downstream cross-entropy
loss and the \(\ell_1\) term on active masks encourages
sparsity. We set \(\lambda = 5\times10^{-4}\) in all experiments.
The model is fine-tuned with AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} at a
learning rate of \(3\times10^{-5}\) for 20\,000 steps on
\dataset{}-train.

\subsection{Architecture Overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the \modelname{} pipeline. A
pre-trained vision encoder (ViT-L/14) and a language encoder
(BERT-Large) independently produce token sequences that are routed
through the adaptive mask module before being fed into a shared
cross-modal transformer. Pruned tokens are dropped from the attention
computation; their positions are passed to a lightweight \emph{residual
injector} that re-inserts coarse information for final projection.
