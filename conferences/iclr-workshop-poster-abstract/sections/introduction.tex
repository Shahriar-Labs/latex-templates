\section{Introduction}

The rise of large vision--language models (VLMs) such as
\citet{radford2021clip} and \citet{dosovitskiy2021vit} has transformed
multimodal understanding, enabling systems that jointly reason over
images and natural language. Yet the quadratic complexity of
self-attention with respect to sequence length makes full-attention
inference prohibitively expensive when both visual and textual token
streams are long.

Existing efficiency approaches fall into three categories:
\textit{structured pruning} of model weights
\citep{he2016resnet}, \textit{quantisation} of activations and weights,
and \textit{token reduction}---the focus of this work.
Token-reduction methods discard or merge tokens deemed unimportant for
the final prediction, but prior work~\citep{chen2021simclr} largely
treats modality-specific and cross-modal tokens identically and
applies fixed, hand-crafted rules that do not generalise across tasks.

\textbf{Our contribution.}\quad We present \modelname{}, a
\emph{modality-aware adaptive token routing} framework that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=(\roman*), itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item Learns separate sparsity masks for vision and language tokens
        using a differentiable top-\(k\) operator, allowing end-to-end
        training with a standard cross-entropy objective plus a
        FLOPs-regularisation term.
  \item Achieves \textbf{3.1\(\times\)} FLOPs reduction and
        \textbf{2.4\(\times\)} latency reduction with
        \(\leq\)0.5\% accuracy drop across twelve \dataset{} tasks.
  \item Transfers sparsity masks across model scales (ViT-B, ViT-L,
        ViT-H) and language backbones without retraining.
\end{enumerate}

This work targets practitioners deploying VLMs in latency-sensitive
environments such as on-device assistants and real-time visual
question answering. We believe the learned-mask paradigm opens a
practical path to sub-quadratic multimodal inference without
architectural surgery.
