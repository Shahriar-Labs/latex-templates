\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}

We evaluate on two session benchmarks. \textbf{NexaQL} is a sample of 2.4M sessions
from a general web search log, stratified so that 40\% of test-set queries are
long-tail (fewer than five historical occurrences). \textbf{MeridianSessions} is a
smaller, denser benchmark of 310K sessions drawn from a vertical shopping search
engine, used to test transfer to a narrower domain. Both are split temporally into
train/validation/test with a two-week gap to avoid session leakage, following the
protocol of \citet{Delacroix2020Evaluation}.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare against \textbf{BM25} query expansion, \textbf{RankNet-Session}
\citep{Whitfield2016RankNet} adapted to session features, \textbf{GraphRank}
\citep{Rajan2021GraphRank}, and \textbf{LongTail-BERT}, a transformer bi-encoder
fine-tuned with the contrastive pretraining recipe of
\citet{Lindqvist2020Contrastive}. All baselines are retuned on our splits with the
same candidate shortlist as CSG for a fair comparison.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

The graph encoder uses hidden dimension $d{=}256$, $L{=}3$ relational graph
convolution layers, and dropout $0.1$. We train with Adam at learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, batch size $512$ sessions, and temperature $\tau$ initialized at
$0.07$ and learned. Training runs for $20$ epochs with early stopping on validation
NDCG@10; all reported results are averaged over five seeds.
