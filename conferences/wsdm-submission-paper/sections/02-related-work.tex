\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Click models and position bias.} Cascade-style click models
\citep{Feld2017ClickModel} and their debiased extensions \citep{Sundqvist2015Debiasing}
remain the dominant tool for turning implicit feedback into training signal, but they
are typically fit per-query and degrade sharply when a query is rare or unseen, which
is precisely the regime we target.

\paragraph{Session-based retrieval and reformulation.} Sequence models over query
histories \citep{Feldman2018Embeddings} and reinforcement-signal reformulation
\citep{Csikos2021Reformulation} both use session context, but treat the session as a
flat sequence rather than a graph with explicit click and reformulation edges. Graph
neural approaches to search have mostly targeted auto-completion
\citep{Bauer2019GNN} or general-purpose ranking \citep{Rajan2021GraphRank} rather than
reformulation specifically.

\paragraph{Contrastive representation learning for IR.} Contrastive pretraining has
improved dense query and document representations \citep{Lindqvist2020Contrastive}
and transformer-based retrieval under sparsity \citep{Marsh2022BertRetrieval}, but
these methods operate at the query-pair level and do not exploit the graph structure
of an entire session, which is the gap CSG addresses.

\paragraph{Long-tail modeling and evaluation.} \citet{Okonkwo2018LongTail} document
the extent of long-tail sparsity in production search logs, and
\citet{Delacroix2020Evaluation} caution against offline evaluation protocols that
implicitly favor head-heavy test splits; we adopt their stratified evaluation
recommendation in \S\ref{sec:experiments}. Pairwise ranking losses
\citep{Whitfield2016RankNet} and skip-gram query embeddings
\citep{Feldman2018Embeddings} form two of our baselines.
