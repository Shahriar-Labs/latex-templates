\section{Binding and Composition}
\label{sec:binding}

Binding reduces to finding two distinct random-oracle inputs with the same
output.  The reduction is unusually direct because verification recomputes
the full canonical encoding.

\begin{theorem}[Binding]
\label{thm:binding}
For an adversary making at most $q_H$ distinct oracle queries,
\[
  \Adv^{\mathsf{bind}}_\Pi(\adv)
  \leq \binom{q_H+2}{2}2^{-\tau}+\varepsilon_{\mathsf{enc}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Two accepted, unequal openings for the same context yield distinct canonical
inputs $X\neq X'$ by \cref{lem:context-separation}, but verification requires
$\Hash(X)=C=\Hash(X')$.  Across at most $q_H+2$ evaluated inputs, the birthday
bound for independent $\tau$-bit outputs gives the stated probability.  A
non-canonical parser is represented by $\varepsilon_{\mathsf{enc}}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why every context field matters}

The encoding is not decorative.  Removing fields changes the theorem that can
be claimed.

\secpar{No protocol tag} Suppose a ballot protocol and a credential protocol
both hash $(sid,i,m,r)$ with the same oracle.  A commitment produced for one is
byte-for-byte valid in the other.  Neither local binding nor local hiding is
violated, yet cross-protocol replay is possible.

\secpar{No session identifier} If positions restart at one in every session,
a commitment and its later opening can be transplanted to another session.
An application-level freshness check may stop the replay, but that check then
becomes a missing hypothesis of the cryptographic composition theorem.

\secpar{No position} Swapping commitments inside a vector preserves validity.
This may be acceptable for an unordered set, but not for a ballot indexed by
contest or a proof whose coordinates have different meanings.  Including $i$
also makes the oracle inputs distinct even when messages and salts repeat.

\secpar{Ambiguous concatenation} Raw concatenation permits tuples such as
$(\mathtt{a},\mathtt{bc})$ and $(\mathtt{ab},\mathtt{c})$ to share an input.
Length prefixes or a canonical serialization format are necessary for the
injectivity used in both main proofs.
