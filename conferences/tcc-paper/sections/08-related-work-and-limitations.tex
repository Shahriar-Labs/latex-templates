\section{Related Work and Limitations}
\label{sec:related}

Commitment schemes originate in foundational work on cryptographic protocol
design, including Blum's coin-flipping methodology~\cite{Blum1983}.  The
random-oracle model provides a widely used abstraction for hash-based proofs
\cite{BellareRogaway1993}, while Canetti, Goldreich, and Halevi emphasize that
secure ROM proofs do not automatically instantiate securely with every
concrete hash function~\cite{CanettiEtAl1998}.  Our theorem should be read with
that methodological limitation in mind.

Selective opening was isolated in the context of concurrent and composable
protocols by Dwork, Naor, Reingold, and Stockmeyer~\cite{DworkEtAl2003}.
Subsequent work developed simulation-based definitions and constructions under
standard assumptions~\cite{BellareHofheinzYilek2012}.  Those results address
more general message distributions and definitions than ours.  We instead
make a ROM entropy argument explicit and connect it to domain-separated
serialization.

Our analysis has four principal limitations.  First, residual entropy is a
strong, application-dependent hypothesis; opening one coordinate of a secret-
sharing or replicated vector may destroy it.  Second, message length is public.
Third, classical lazy sampling does not cover superposition queries to a
quantum random oracle~\cite{Unruh2015}.  Fourth, the construction provides no
equivocation against a party that already queried the eventual opening point.
Applications needing universally composable commitments, extraction, or
arbitrary correlated-message selective opening should use a construction
proved for that stronger target.
