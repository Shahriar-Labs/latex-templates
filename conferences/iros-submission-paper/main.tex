\begin{abstract}
Quadrupedal robots traversing rubble, loose gravel, and staircases frequently mis-place
footholds when relying on elevation maps alone, since map noise near occlusion boundaries
corrupts the terrain estimate exactly where safe contact points are scarcest. We present
\textsc{ContactRefine}, a lightweight foothold refinement module that consumes a coarse
elevation map together with per-leg contact force history and outputs a corrected stance
point within a $4\,\text{cm}$ search radius of the planner's nominal foothold. The module is
trained with a contrastive objective that pulls refined footholds toward regions of
historically low slip and pushes them away from geometry that previously produced contact-
force spikes. Across $1{,}400$ trials on a physical quadruped crossing five terrain classes
(rubble, loose gravel, wet tile, staircases, and root-covered trail), \textsc{ContactRefine}
reduces the stumble rate from $17.9\%$ to $6.2\%$ and lowers mean cost of transport by
$11.4\%$ relative to a strong elevation-map-only baseline, while adding only
$1.8\,\text{ms}$ of latency to the $500\,\text{Hz}$ control loop.
\end{abstract}
