\section{Experiments}
We evaluate HT-CTS on four synthetic linear contextual bandit environments (Table~\ref{tab:envs}) with tail index $\epsilon$ ranging from $1.0$ (finite variance) down to $0.3$ (very heavy tails). Rewards are generated as $r_t = \langle \theta^\star, x_{t,a} \rangle + \eta_t$ with $\eta_t$ drawn from a Student-$t$ distribution whose degrees of freedom are set to match the target $(1+\epsilon)$-moment bound $u$. Contexts are sampled uniformly from the unit ball, and $\theta^\star$ is fixed per environment with $\|\theta^\star\|_2 = 1$.

We compare HT-CTS against four baselines: \textbf{UCB1}, applied to a linear-arm discretization; \textbf{Gaussian Thompson Sampling (TS-G)}, the standard Bayesian linear-regression variant; \textbf{LinUCB} \citep{lindqvist2019linucb}; and \textbf{$\epsilon$-Greedy} with a decaying exploration rate, following the analysis of \citet{sterling2020epsilon}. All methods share the same ridge prior $\sigma_0^2 = 1$ and are run for $T = 20{,}000$ rounds, averaged over $50$ random seeds; we report mean cumulative regret with standard error.
