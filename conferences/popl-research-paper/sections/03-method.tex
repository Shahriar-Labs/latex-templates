\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Cost Monoid and Judgments}

\textsc{Bidir} is parametrized by a commutative cost monoid
$(\mathcal{C}, \oplus, \mathbf{0})$ equipped with a preorder
$\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{C}}$ that is monotone in $\oplus$. Instantiating
$\mathcal{C}$ to $(\mathbb{N}, +, 0)$ with the usual order yields an
allocation-count budget; instantiating it to $(\mathbb{N}, \max, 0)$
yields a heap high-water-mark budget. The discipline is stated once,
against the abstract monoid, and specialized only at the top level.

The system has two judgments. The \emph{synthesis} judgment
$\Gamma \vdash e \Rightarrow \tau \mid c$ says that, under context
$\Gamma$, expression $e$ synthesizes type $\tau$ with cost $c$. The
\emph{checking} judgment $\Gamma \vdash e \Leftarrow \tau \mid c$ says
that $e$ checks against expected type $\tau$ within budget $c$. The two
are connected by the usual bidirectional subsumption rule, extended with
a cost-side check:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:subsumption}
  \frac{
    \Gamma \vdash e \Rightarrow \tau' \mid c' \qquad
    \tau' \le \tau \qquad
    c' \sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{C}} c
  }{
    \Gamma \vdash e \Leftarrow \tau \mid c
  }
  \;\;\textsc{(Subsume)}
\end{equation}

Function application synthesizes by combining the callee's synthesized
cost, the argument's checked cost against the callee's declared
parameter budget, and a constant $c_{\mathrm{app}}$ for the call itself:
$\Gamma \vdash e_1\,e_2 \Rightarrow \tau_2 \mid c_1 \oplus c_2 \oplus
c_{\mathrm{app}}$, where $\Gamma \vdash e_1 \Rightarrow \tau_1 \to^{c_a}
\tau_2 \mid c_1$ and $\Gamma \vdash e_2 \Leftarrow \tau_1 \mid c_2$ with
$c_2 \sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{C}} c_a$.

\subsection{Annotation Placement}

An abstraction $\lambda x.\,e$ \emph{synthesizes} a cost arrow type
$\tau_1 \to^{c} \tau_2$ by checking $e$ against $\tau_2$ under a
budget $c$ that is itself synthesized bottom-up from $e$'s subterms,
\emph{provided} the abstraction is not stored in a recursive binding,
passed across a signature boundary, or captured in a data structure. In
each of those three boundary cases, \textsc{Bidir} instead requires the
programmer to supply an explicit annotation $\tau_1 \to^{\hat{c}} \tau_2$,
against which the body is checked; this is the only point at which the
declarative system consults a user-written cost term.

\subsection{Soundness and Completeness}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Soundness.} If $\Gamma \vdash e \Leftarrow \tau \mid c$ is
    derivable in the algorithmic system, then $\Gamma \vdash e \Leftarrow
    \tau \mid c$ is derivable in the declarative system, by induction on
    the algorithmic derivation and monotonicity of $\oplus$ in
    $\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{C}}$.
  \item \emph{Completeness.} If $\Gamma \vdash e \Leftarrow \tau \mid c$
    is derivable declaratively and every boundary abstraction in $e$
    carries its required annotation, the algorithm synthesizes some
    $c' \sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{C}} c$, by induction on typing derivations
    with a case split on \textsc{Subsume}.
\end{itemize}

Full proofs, including the well-founded induction measure used for the
recursive-binding case, are given in the extended version.
