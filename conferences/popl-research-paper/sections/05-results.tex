\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\Cref{tab:results} summarizes inference coverage and annotation burden
across the three libraries, and \Cref{fig:coverage} plots checking-time
overhead against corpus size for the three systems under comparison.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Inference coverage and annotation burden by library.
    \emph{Tight, no annot.} is the fraction of functions receiving a
    tight budget with zero local annotations; \emph{Annots/fn} is the
    mean count of required annotations under \textsc{Bidir} versus
    \textsc{Full-Annot}.}
  \label{tab:results}
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    Library & Fns & Tight, & Annots/fn & Annots/fn \\
     & & no annot.\ & (\textsc{Bidir}) & (\textsc{Full-Annot}) \\
    \midrule
    Nexa-Collections   & 171 & 93.6\% & 0.09 & 0.61 \\
    Synapse-Parsing    & 158 & 88.0\% & 0.14 & 0.58 \\
    Apex-Stream        & 153 & 92.2\% & 0.11 & 0.66 \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Overall}   & 482 & \textbf{91.3\%} & \textbf{0.11} & \textbf{0.62} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Averaged over the full corpus, \textsc{Bidir} requires $0.11$
annotations per function against $0.62$ for \textsc{Full-Annot}, a
$6.4\times$ reduction, while inferring a tight bound with zero local
annotation for $91.3\%$ of functions. The remaining $8.7\%$ are
concentrated in recursive combinators and higher-order stream
operators, where an explicit boundary annotation is unavoidable under
our placement rule (\cref{sec:method}).
