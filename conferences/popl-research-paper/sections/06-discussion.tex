\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The gap between \textsc{Bidir}'s $91.3\%$ no-annotation coverage and
\textsc{Full-Annot}'s trivial $100\%$ annotation baseline is explained
almost entirely by the boundary placement rule of
\cref{sec:method}: any $\lambda$-abstraction that is stored, exported
across a signature, or captured recursively needs a signature regardless
of how simple its body is. We view this as a feature rather than a
limitation --- it means every annotation \textsc{Bidir} does ask for is
one that documents an actual interface, not an incidental syntactic
artifact of writing a closure. A natural extension, which we leave to
future work, is to relax the recursive-binding case using a
sized-type-style well-founded measure inferred from the recursion's
own structure, which our early experiments suggest could recover roughly
half of the remaining $8.7\%$ without a corresponding soundness cost.
The $1.8\times$ median checking overhead is dominated by the
subtyping-style comparison $\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{C}}$ at each
application site; a memoized variant of this comparison is the most
promising target for closing the gap with \textsc{Full-Annot}'s $1.19\times$
overhead observed in \Cref{fig:coverage}.
