    \textbf{Abstract.} Automated program repair (APR) pipelines routinely generate dozens of candidate patches per bug, only a small fraction of which are both test-passing and semantically correct. We present \textsc{RankGen}, a retrieval-augmented ranking layer that scores candidate patches by combining (i) similarity to a corpus of previously accepted human patches, (ii) the log-likelihood assigned by a masked span-infilling language model, and (iii) a lightweight overfitting prior estimated from static patch features. RankGen sits downstream of any candidate generator and requires no change to the underlying repair search. Across three benchmarks spanning 412 real-world defects, RankGen improves the correct-patch rate within a top-10 budget by 12.4 percentage points on average over a strong retrieval-free baseline, while adding under 3 seconds of ranking latency per bug. We release our scoring formulation, ablations over each score component, and an analysis of failure modes where retrieval similarity misleads the ranker.
    \par\vspace{0.3em}
    \textit{Keywords:} automated program repair, retrieval-augmented generation, patch ranking, empirical software engineering
