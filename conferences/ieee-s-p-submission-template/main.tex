\begin{abstract}
Confidential virtual machines (CVMs) built on AMD SEV-SNP and Corex TDX encrypt guest memory
and registers, but the last-level cache (LLC) they share with co-resident tenants remains an
observable, unencrypted channel. We present \sysname, a hypervisor-side detector that flags
cross-VM cache contention attacks -- Prime+Probe and Flush+Reload style probing -- without
instrumenting, pausing, or reading anything inside the victim CVM. \sysname{} samples
per-core hardware performance counters, turns counter deltas into a lightweight contention
feature, smooths the feature with a streaming exponentially-weighted classifier, and responds
by re-pinning the suspected attacker's virtual core rather than migrating or killing the
victim. Across three production-like host traces, \sysname{} detects 94.6\% of injected
attack sessions at a 1.8\% false-positive rate and 2.1\% victim throughput overhead,
dominating a reimplementation of \baseline{} at every overhead budget we tested and
responding to a sustained attack in a median of 340\,ms.
\end{abstract}
