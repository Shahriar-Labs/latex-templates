\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Fiat and Shamir introduced the heuristic transformation from identification to
signatures~\cite{FiatShamir1986}.  Bellare and Rogaway formalized the random
oracle as a design and analysis methodology~\cite{BellareRogaway1993}, while
Pointcheval and Stern developed the forking technique for security proofs of
signature schemes~\cite{PointchevalStern1996}.  Schnorr's discrete-logarithm
identification and signature construction is the canonical algebraic example
of the transform~\cite{Schnorr1991}.

Later work sharpened the understanding of Fiat--Shamir in both classical and
quantum settings.  Abdalla, An, Bellare, and Namprempre studied transformations
and security notions for identification schemes~\cite{AbdallaEtAl2002}.
Unruh gave a quantum-resistant transform for proofs of knowledge
~\cite{Unruh2015}, and Don, Fehr, Majenz, and Schaffner developed a
measure-and-reprogram technique for QROM proofs~\cite{DonEtAl2019}.  Kiltz,
Lyubashevsky, and Schaffner analyzed the concrete security of Fiat--Shamir
signatures under active attacks~\cite{KiltzEtAl2018}.  Our narrower focus is
the transparent separation of multi-user encoding and salt-related bad events
from whichever extraction result is plugged into the final step.
