\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}

The proof has two independent components.  First, salts control collisions
among points that the simulator chooses.  Second, a forking step obtains a
second challenge for the adversary's decisive oracle query.

\begin{lemma}[Salt-collision bound]
  \label{lem:collision}
  Among at most $q_P$ honest proof queries, the probability that two queries
  with identical $(pk,x,a)$ also select the same salt is at most
  \[
     \delta_{\mathrm{salt}} \le \frac{q_P(q_P-1)}{2^{\rho+1}}.
  \]
  The same bound holds if the adversary adaptively selects the public key and
  instance before each salt is sampled.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Condition on the entire execution before a salt is sampled.  For each pair
  of proof queries with matching prefixes $(pk,x,a)$, the later independent
  salt equals the earlier one with probability $2^{-\rho}$.  There are at most
  $\binom{q_P}{2}$ pairs.  Adaptivity changes which pairs have matching
  prefixes but not the conditional probability for their fresh salts.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pre-query bound]
  \label{lem:prequery}
  Suppose an honest proof's commitment $a$ is fixed before its salt is sampled.
  For any adversary making at most $q_H$ oracle queries overall, the probability
  that its table already contains
  $\mathsf{Enc}(pk,x,a,s)$ when a fresh salt $s$ is sampled is at most
  $q_H/2^\rho$ per proof query.  Thus
  $\Prob[\mathsf{PreQ}]\le q_Pq_H/2^\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  After conditioning on $(pk,x,a)$ and the adversary's current view, at most
  $q_H$ table entries can have the required prefix.  Each determines at most
  one bad $\rho$-bit salt by injectivity of the encoding.  A union bound over
  the $q_P$ proof queries completes the argument.
\end{proof}

To state the extraction term cleanly, let $\varepsilon$ be the adversary's
success probability after excluding the two bad events above.  A standard
forking experiment reruns $\adv$ with identical random tape and identical
oracle answers before its decisive query, then supplies an independent
challenge at that query.  Write $q=q_H+q_P+1$ for an upper bound on the number
of candidate transcript-defining oracle points, including those created while
answering proof queries.

\begin{theorem}[Multi-user extraction]
  \label{thm:main}
  Let $\Pi$ have challenge space of size $2^t$ and special-soundness error
  $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ss}}$.  For every classical adversary $\adv$ against
  $\trans^{\hash}_{\Pi,\rho}$, there is a black-box extractor $\bdv$ such that
  \begin{align}
  \Adv^{\mathrm{muk}}_{\trans,\adv}
  \;&\le
  \sqrt{q\bigl(\Adv^{\mathrm{ext}}_{\Pi,\bdv}
             +2^{-t}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ss}}\bigr)}
  + \frac{q_Pq_H}{2^\rho}
  + \frac{q_P(q_P-1)}{2^{\rho+1}},
  \label{eq:main-bound}
  \end{align}
  where $\Time(\bdv)$ is at most two executions of $\adv$ plus
  $O(q)$ table operations and one invocation of the special-soundness
  extractor.  The bound has no additional multiplicative factor $U$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  We proceed through games.

  \emph{Game $G_0$.} This is the real multi-user experiment with one global
  lazy-sampled oracle.

  \emph{Game $G_1$.} The challenger answers honest proof requests by choosing
  the salt and programming the transcript-defining oracle point consistently.
  If that point was previously queried, it sets $\mathsf{PreQ}$ and aborts.
  By \cref{lem:prequery}, the statistical difference from $G_0$ is at most
  $q_Pq_H/2^\rho$.

  \emph{Game $G_2$.} Abort when two simulated proofs reuse an oracle point.
  By \cref{lem:collision}, this costs at most
  $q_P(q_P-1)/2^{\rho+1}$.  Conditioned on no abort, every programmed point is
  fresh and has exactly the random-oracle distribution.

  Now fix a successful execution of $G_2$.  Except with probability $2^{-t}$,
  the adversary queried the oracle at
  $X^*=\mathsf{Enc}(pk_{i^*},x^*,a^*,s^*)$ before outputting its proof: without
  that query, its response predicts a uniform challenge.  Select the decisive
  query among at most $q$ candidates and apply the classical forking argument
  of Pointcheval and Stern~\cite{PointchevalStern1996}.  Replaying with the
  same prefix and an independent answer yields, with probability at least
  $\varepsilon^2/q-2^{-t}$, two accepting transcripts sharing
  $(pk_{i^*},x^*,a^*)$ and having distinct challenges.  Applying special
  soundness extracts a witness, apart from
  $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ss}}$.

  The public key is part of $X^*$.  Hence the fork stays in the same user's
  domain by \cref{lem:domains}; the extractor neither guesses $i^*$ in advance
  nor loses a factor $U$.  Rearranging the resulting inequality for
  $\varepsilon$ and adding the two game-transition terms proves
  \cref{eq:main-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What ``no factor $U$'' means]
The adversary's resources $q_H$ and $q_P$ may naturally grow with the number of
users.  The theorem does not make a system with more traffic free.  It states
that after total traffic is fixed, the reduction does not pay a second loss
merely to guess which public key appears in the forgery.
\end{remark}
