\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Deep neural networks (DNNs) have revolutionized cognitive computing tasks, prompting significant interest in high-performance hardware design. General-purpose processors are ill-suited for the computational intensity of DNNs, leading to the rise of specialized accelerators such as Nexa's TPU, Nexa-Core \cite{nexa2024}, and Synapse-V1 \cite{synapse2023}. Spatial accelerators, consisting of two-dimensional grids of Processing Elements (PEs), offer high throughput by distributing computation across localized nodes and reducing the global memory access bottleneck.

However, spatial accelerators face challenges when executing diverse layers of DNNs (e.g., dense layers, sparse convolutions). Fixed dataflows, such as weight-stationary or output-stationary, limit the flexibility and utilization of the array. The Meridian platform \cite{meridian2021} provides a robust emulation framework to evaluate alternative hardware architectures.

In this work, we propose \textbf{Vertex-Flow}, a spatial accelerator with an adaptive dataflow architecture designed to improve execution efficiency. Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We design a reconfigurable PE structure that supports both weight-stationary and hybrid activation-stationary modes.
  \item We introduce an on-chip network routing mechanism to dynamically manage accumulation paths.
  \item We evaluate Vertex-Flow on the Meridian platform and show substantial improvements in latency and power over baseline systems.
\end{itemize}

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section \ref{sec:related} reviews related work. Section \ref{sec:method} presents the proposed Vertex-Flow architecture. Section \ref{sec:setup} details our experimental setup, and Section \ref{sec:results} discusses the results. Section \ref{sec:discussion} provides a design space exploration, and Section \ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.
