\begin{abstract}
Modeling graphs that are simultaneously heterogeneous in node and edge type and dynamic in
time remains difficult: most encoders specialize in one axis of variation and degrade on the
other. We present \sysname{}, a contrastive representation learning framework that jointly
encodes type heterogeneity and temporal dynamics through a type-aware attention module
coupled with a time-decayed neighborhood sampler. \sysname{} is trained with an in-batch
contrastive objective that treats temporally adjacent interactions of the same node as
positive pairs, requiring no labeled supervision at pretraining time. Across three benchmark
graphs spanning e-commerce, social, and citation domains, \sysname{} improves link-prediction
AUC-ROC by 2.1--4.6 points over the strongest baseline while using 31\% fewer parameters. We
release ablations isolating the contribution of type-awareness versus temporal decay, and
discuss failure modes on sparsely connected node types.
\end{abstract}
