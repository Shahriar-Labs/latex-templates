\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Testbed}
We built a 28~GHz phased-array testbed using two development boards with $8\times8$ element arrays, one fixed as an access point and one worn at the chest as the mobile station. Wrist-worn IMU data was streamed over a wired debug link to avoid introducing wireless-scheduling artifacts into the ground-truth timing. Full sweeps covered $K = 64$ codebook entries; narrowed probes covered 3.

\subsection{Participants and Routes}
Eight volunteers ($4$ male, $4$ female, ages 22--41) walked three indoor routes inside our lab building: (R1) a 40~m straight corridor, (R2) a route with four 90\textdegree{} turns, and (R3) an open-plan area with informal, unscripted movement. Each participant completed each route three times, for 72 total trials. All participants gave informed consent under our institution's lab study protocol.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}
We compare three systems: \textbf{Periodic Sweep}, a full $K$-beam sweep every 100~ms regardless of link state; \textbf{IMU-Only Kalman}, a constant-velocity predictor equivalent to dropping the sway term from \eq{eq:beam-predict}~\cite{kowalczyk2021kalman}; and \textbf{TrailBeam (Ours)}. We additionally report a \textbf{Genie-Aided Oracle} that knows the true future angle and always selects the optimal beam, as an upper bound. Our primary metrics are median and 90th-percentile re-alignment latency, link-alignment accuracy (fraction of control intervals with $\mathrm{SNR} \geq \mathrm{SNR}_{\min}$), and energy per re-alignment event, measured with an inline power monitor on the phased-array supply rail.
