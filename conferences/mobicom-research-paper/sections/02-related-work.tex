\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Beam management for mobile mmWave.} Hierarchical and adaptive codebook search reduces the cost of a single sweep~\cite{kastanien2017beamforming}, and surveys of the space catalogue a wide range of exhaustive, hierarchical, and compressive-sensing approaches~\cite{oyelaran2019survey}. These techniques speed up an individual sweep but do not address \emph{when} a sweep should be triggered, which is the question TrailBeam targets.

\textbf{Motion-assisted tracking.} Prior work has used inertial sensing to assist beam tracking on body-worn radios, either by filtering observed angle-of-arrival estimates~\cite{vranic2019imu,kowalczyk2021kalman} or by correlating body sway with link quality~\cite{fennimore2018sway}. These systems are reactive: they smooth a noisy signal but do not forecast periodic motion patterns such as gait. TrailBeam differs by explicitly modeling gait phase, following work on low-latency gait-phase estimation from wrist inertial data~\cite{demirci2020gait}, and using the phase estimate to anticipate the next sway extremum before it occurs.

\textbf{Mobility-aware localization and handover.} Multipath fingerprinting techniques localize commodity smartphones to sub-meter accuracy indoors~\cite{oberholzer2020fingerprint}, and crowd-sensed blockage maps have been used to trigger predictive handover between mmWave access points~\cite{brandsma2022crowd}. These systems predict \emph{macro}-mobility across access points; TrailBeam instead predicts \emph{micro}-mobility of the body relative to a single fixed or co-located radio.

\textbf{Energy-aware wearable radios.} Duty-cycled tracking loops trade tracking accuracy for battery life~\cite{halloran2021energy}, ambient backscatter has been proposed to assist low-power beam recovery~\cite{nakashima2022backscatter}, and edge-offloaded motion models move prediction off the wearable entirely~\cite{lindqvist2023offload}. TrailBeam is complementary to these: its predictor is cheap enough to run on-device, but its re-alignment decisions could be further gated by an energy budget from any of these systems. Recent measurement work has also shown that RF fingerprints themselves drift under pedestrian mobility~\cite{szabo2023rf}, reinforcing the need for motion-explicit, rather than purely RF-explicit, tracking.
