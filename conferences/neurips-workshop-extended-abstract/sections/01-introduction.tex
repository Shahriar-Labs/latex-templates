\section{Introduction}

Models deployed outside their training environment often encounter systematic
distribution shift. Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) addresses this setting
using labelled examples from a source distribution and unlabelled examples from
a related target distribution. Feature-alignment methods can learn
domain-invariant representations~\citep{ganin2016domain,tzeng2017adversarial},
whereas self-training instead treats confident target predictions as temporary
labels~\citep{french2018self,sohn2020fixmatch}. The latter is attractive because
it introduces little architectural overhead and integrates naturally with
modern augmentation pipelines.

The central weakness of self-training is confirmation bias: early mistakes are
reintroduced as supervision and become increasingly confident. A single global
acceptance threshold is particularly brittle under target shift. It can reject
most examples from hard or rare classes while accepting overconfident errors
from easy classes. Moreover, neural confidence scores are often
miscalibrated~\citep{guo2017calibration}, so a score of $0.9$ need not correspond
to a $90\%$ chance of correctness.

We study whether reliability can be improved without target labels or a learned
domain discriminator. Our approach, \method, combines online
class-conditional thresholds with consistency under perturbation. The method
has three practical properties: (i) it changes only the training loss;
(ii) it adds no parameters at inference; and (iii) it provides a direct control
over pseudo-label coverage. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a confidence gate whose class-specific thresholds adapt to the observed
        target prediction distribution;
  \item an agreement test that suppresses unstable pseudo-labels under strong
        augmentation; and
  \item an empirical analysis separating accuracy, calibration, coverage, and
        class balance across several domain shifts.
\end{itemize}
