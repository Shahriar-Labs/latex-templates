\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Pseudo-label self-training is effective for unsupervised domain adaptation,
but a fixed confidence threshold can amplify errors when target-domain classes
differ in difficulty or frequency. We introduce \method, a lightweight
self-training objective that accepts a pseudo-label only when two conditions
hold: the prediction is calibrated above a class-conditional threshold, and
weak and strong augmentations agree. Thresholds are updated online from
exponentially smoothed target statistics, requiring neither target labels nor
an auxiliary discriminator. On three standard image-domain adaptation suites,
\method{} improves mean target accuracy over fixed-threshold self-training
while reducing expected calibration error. Controlled ablations indicate that
agreement filtering and class-conditional calibration are complementary. These
results suggest that explicitly modelling pseudo-label reliability can provide
a simple, stable alternative to increasingly elaborate adaptation modules.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} domain adaptation, self-training, pseudo-labels,
uncertainty calibration, distribution shift
