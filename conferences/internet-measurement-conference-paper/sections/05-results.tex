\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Table~\ref{tab:results} summarizes per-technology LIR and loss statistics aggregated over
the full campaign. Fiber access shows both the lowest median inflation ($0.06$) and the
tightest tail ($p95 = 0.19$), consistent with symmetric, lightly buffered PON access
architectures and comparatively little contention among subscribers sharing a given
optical distribution point. Cable and DSL cluster together at roughly $3$--$4\times$
fiber's median inflation, which we attribute to a combination of larger static buffers in
deployed cable modems and, for DSL, older CPE that in our panel rarely had modern AQM
enabled. Fixed wireless, satellite, and mobile technologies show the heaviest tails: LEO
satellite's median LIR ($0.29$) is comparable to cable, but its 95th percentile ($1.35$)
and loss rate ($1.86\%$) are the highest of any technology we measured, reflecting
episodic contention and handoff effects between satellite beams.

Figure~\ref{fig:cdf} shows the full empirical distribution of per-probe median LIR for
three representative technologies. The fiber curve rises steeply and saturates below
$\mathrm{LIR}=0.3$, indicating that severe inflation is rare rather than merely
low on average. Cable's distribution is shifted right and has a visibly longer tail,
and fixed wireless shows the flattest, widest distribution of the three, with a
non-trivial fraction of probes exceeding $\mathrm{LIR}=1.0$ even at the median-aggregation
level shown here.
