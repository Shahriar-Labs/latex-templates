\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Early work on queueing delay in home networks established that consumer routers and
cable/DSL modems frequently ship with oversized, statically configured buffers, producing
latency spikes far larger than would be predicted by link capacity and offered load
alone~\cite{whitfield2017bufferbloat}. Subsequent audits of AQM deployment in shipped home
gateways found that, even where AQM algorithms were available, default configurations
frequently left them disabled~\cite{bellini2016aqm}. Our own prior work examined last-mile
latency in a ten-country panel but was limited to idle-network measurements and did not
isolate the loaded-latency contribution that is the focus of the present
study~\cite{marchetti2019lastmile}.

A parallel line of work has focused on measurement methodology itself.
Bauer and Solvang survey active-probing techniques for residential broadband and catalog
the confounds that arise from cross-traffic, NAT middleboxes, and CPE-level
scheduling~\cite{bauer2015probing}; we adopt several of their recommended controls,
including paired idle/loaded sampling and per-probe outlier filtering. Rahman and Wei
decompose end-to-end web latency across the access path and show that access-link
queueing, rather than core-network or server-side delay, dominates the variance in
last-mile response time for a majority of residential connections~\cite{rahman2018cdn}.

Technology-specific measurement has grown alongside the diversification of residential
access. Sundaram and Natarajan characterize fixed wireless access performance across
spectrum bands and find substantial variance tied to sector loading~\cite{sundaram2020fwa}.
Ferreira and Petrova provide one of the first broad looks at LEO satellite latency and
jitter, reporting idle latencies far lower than legacy geostationary satellite but
significant variability under contention~\cite{ferreira2021satellite}. Okonkwo and
Whitfield track DSL performance longitudinally as fiber overbuild reduces the subscriber
base sharing legacy copper plant~\cite{okonkwo2021dsl}, and Natarajan and Okonkwo measure
cellular latency under load across 4G and early 5G macro deployments~\cite{natarajan2019mobile}.
Petrova and Bellini connect access-link delay directly to interactive quality-of-experience
outcomes~\cite{petrova2018qoe}, and Wei and Zhao report early field signals from L4S
deployment trials~\cite{wei2022l4s}. Our study differs from this body of work by measuring
a consistent metric across all six access technologies within a single, contemporaneous
panel, and at a scale that supports cross-technology and cross-ISP comparison.
