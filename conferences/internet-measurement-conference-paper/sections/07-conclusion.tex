\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a six-week, multi-vantage active-measurement study of loaded-latency
behavior across six residential access technologies, introducing the Latency Inflation
Ratio as a simple, comparable metric for quantifying access-link queueing delay under
realistic load. Across 3{,}116 vantage points in 41 ISPs and 12 countries, we find that
fiber access delivers substantially lower and more consistent loaded latency than cable,
DSL, fixed wireless, satellite, or mobile access, and that fixed wireless and satellite
technologies in particular exhibit heavy-tailed inflation that is not well summarized by
median statistics alone. We hope this dataset and methodology support both future
measurement work and more latency-aware benchmarking of residential access quality.
