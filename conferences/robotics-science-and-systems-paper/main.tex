    \parbox{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\fboxsep\relax}{%
      \small\textbf{Abstract---}
      Perceptive legged robots often fail after a camera is occluded, a depth return is
      missing, or a moving obstacle invalidates the local elevation map. We introduce
      \method{}, a compact terrain memory that separates geometric state from calibrated
      epistemic risk and exposes both to a constrained foothold controller. A recurrent
      fusion module updates a robot-centric map from asynchronous depth and proprioceptive
      observations; a risk head predicts whether each cell is stale, unobserved, or
      inconsistent with recent contact. The controller then trades progress against
      collision probability rather than treating every reconstructed height as equally
      trustworthy. In an illustrative benchmark spanning 1,200 simulated courses and 48
      hardware trials, \method{} reduces intervention rate from 21.7\% to 7.9\% under
      structured sensor dropout while retaining 92\% of nominal traversal speed. Ablations
      show that calibrated risk, contact-driven correction, and temporal memory each account
      for distinct gains. These results support uncertainty as a first-class planning signal
      for reliable mobility beyond continuously visible terrain.

      \vspace{3pt}
      \textbf{Keywords---}legged robots, terrain mapping, uncertainty calibration,
      model-predictive control, sensor dropout.}}
