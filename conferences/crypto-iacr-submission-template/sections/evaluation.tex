\section{Performance Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We implemented a prototype of the Vertex protocol in C++17, compiling with GCC 11.2 and utilizing the NTL mathematical library for polynomial ring operations. We executed our benchmarks on a Synapse database node running on an Intel Xeon Platinum processor at 3.2 GHz with 64 GB RAM.

\subsection{Comparative Performance}
We compare Vertex with state-of-the-art lattice-based signature and ZK systems: Nexa-ZK~\cite{nexa2024lattice}, Apex-Prove~\cite{apex2025signatures}, and Nimbus-Ring~\cite{nimbus2023ring}. \Cref{tab:comparison} details the proof size, prover computational time, verifier time, and the underlying mathematical assumptions.

\begin{table}[htb]
\caption{Performance and security comparison of ZK proof schemes. All times are measured in milliseconds. The parameters target a 128-bit security level.}
\label{tab:comparison}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Scheme} & \textbf{Size (KB)} & \textbf{Prover (ms)} & \textbf{Verifier (ms)} & \textbf{Assumption} \\
  \midrule
  Nexa-ZK~\cite{nexa2024lattice} & 45.2 & 124.0 & 12.4 & Ring-SIS \\
  Apex-Prove~\cite{apex2025signatures} & 38.1 & 98.5 & 8.2 & LWE \\
  Nimbus-Ring~\cite{nimbus2023ring} & 29.4 & 84.1 & 5.1 & Module-LWE \\
  \textbf{Vertex (Ours)} & \textbf{17.2} & \textbf{24.6} & \textbf{1.4} & \textbf{Ring-LWE} \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

As shown in \Cref{tab:comparison}, Vertex significantly outperforms prior solutions. The prover time is reduced by more than 70\% due to the decoupled pipeline design, which avoids evaluating complex multi-dimensional commitments during proof synthesis. The verification latency of 1.4 ms satisfies the strict sub-2ms query constraints required for high-throughput Synapse database clusters.
