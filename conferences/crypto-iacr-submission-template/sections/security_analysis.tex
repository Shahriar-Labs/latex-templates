\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security}

We prove the security properties of the Vertex argument system under the hardness of the Module-SIS and Module-LWE assumptions.

\subsection{Completeness}
We state the completeness of Vertex below.
\begin{theorem}[Completeness]
  If the Prover $\mathcal{P}$ is honest and holds a valid witness $\vectorbf{w}$ such that $\|\vectorbf{w}\|_2 \le \beta$, then the interactive protocol in \Cref{fig:vertex_protocol} succeeds with probability at least $1/e$, where $e$ is the base of the natural logarithm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Assuming the protocol does not abort during the rejection sampling step, we evaluate the verification relation.
  By substitution:
  \begin{align}
    \vectorbf{A}\vectorbf{z} - c \vectorbf{t}_0 &= \vectorbf{A}(\vectorbf{y} + c \cdot \vectorbf{w}) - c(\vectorbf{A}\vectorbf{w}) \\
    &= \vectorbf{A}\vectorbf{y} + c\vectorbf{A}\vectorbf{w} - c\vectorbf{A}\vectorbf{w} \\
    &= \vectorbf{A}\vectorbf{y} = \vectorbf{t}.
  \end{align}
  The hash check evaluates correctly since $\vectorbf{c}_0 = \mathsf{Hash}(\vectorbf{t})$. By adjusting the rejection sampling parameters, we ensure that the response distribution is independent of the secret witness $\vectorbf{w}$ and bounded by $\gamma \cdot \sqrt{d}$ with high probability. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Soundness and Zero-Knowledge}
We model the security of Vertex in the standard game-based framework. \Cref{fig:cpa_game} defines the indistinguishability game under chosen-plaintext attacks for the underlying encryption scheme integrated into Vertex.

The Zero-Knowledge property is established by showing that there exists a simulator $\mathcal{S}$ that can produce transcripts indistinguishable from real protocol runs without access to the witness $\vectorbf{w}$.
