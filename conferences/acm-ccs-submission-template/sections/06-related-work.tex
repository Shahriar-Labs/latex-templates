\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Leakage-abuse attacks.} Islam et al.~\cite{islam2012access}
first demonstrated that access-pattern leakage from searchable encryption
schemes could be exploited via keyword co-occurrence to recover query
plaintexts with high confidence. \citet{kellaris2016generic} generalized
this to range queries, showing that volume leakage alone --- without any
access-pattern information --- suffices to reconstruct an ordered domain
under a persistent adversary, which is the attack family we instantiate in
\S\ref{sec:leakage}. \citet{grubbs2018pumpup} extended reconstruction
attacks to order-revealing encryption deployments, showing that the
relative-order leakage such schemes are built to provide is itself
sufficient for near-complete range recovery; our Apex Store baseline
reproduces this weakness. \sysname{} differs from this line of work in
being a defense evaluated against the same attack family rather than a new
attack.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Oblivious storage.} Path ORAM~\cite{stefanov2018path} and
its successors eliminate access-pattern leakage down to a
poly-logarithmic bandwidth overhead by continuously re-shuffling and
re-encrypting the entire storage tree on every access. This gives a
strictly stronger guarantee than \sysname{} provides, at the cost of
requiring the storage layer itself to implement the ORAM protocol ---
which rules out deployment in front of an unmodified commercial backend,
the setting \sysname{} targets. \sysname{} can be seen as trading a
constant-factor reduction in throughput overhead against a weaker,
quantifiable leakage bound rather than an asymptotically vanishing one.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Padding-based countermeasures.} Prior padding schemes for
encrypted search~\cite{grubbs2018pumpup} typically use a fixed, workload-
independent bucket schedule, which our Vertex Store baseline models. Our
evaluation in \S\ref{sec:evaluation} quantifies a gap in this line of work
that is often noted only informally: padding volumes without also
decorrelating request timing and dispatch order leaves a persistent
adversary with most of the correlation signal padding was meant to
remove, since the identity of \emph{which} padded request corresponds to
which recurring query is untouched by volume padding alone.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Differentially private query interfaces.} An orthogonal
line of work injects noise into query \emph{results} to bound what a
client can learn about the underlying data~\cite{narayan2019dp}. This
protects against a different adversary --- a querying client, rather than
an operator observing the access pattern of a legitimate query --- and is
complementary to \sysname{}: a deployment concerned with both threats
could layer a differentially private query interface on top of the
\sysname{} gateway without conflict, since the two mechanisms act on
disjoint parts of the request/response path.
