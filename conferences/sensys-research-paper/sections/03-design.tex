\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the control loop.  Every observation
updates a predictor and an error scale.  Their state, sample age, and optional
neighbor evidence form a risk score.  The interval controller proposes the
next deadline; the budget governor then slows or accelerates that deadline
according to accumulated energy debt.  A watchdog enforces hard safety bounds.

\subsection{Compact uncertainty model}

For a scalar scheduling feature $x_t$, the node uses a constant-velocity state
$z_t=[x_t,\dot{x}_t]^\mathsf{T}$.  Between irregular observations separated by
$\Delta t$, it predicts

\begin{equation}
\hat{z}_{t|t^-}=
\begin{bmatrix}1&\Delta t\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\hat{z}_{t^-},\qquad
P_{t|t^-}=A P_{t^-}A^\mathsf{T}+Q(\Delta t).
\end{equation}

The observation update is the usual two-state Kalman correction, but \system
adapts measurement scale from the clipped residual
$r_t=x_t-\hat{x}_{t|t^-}$.  With forgetting factor $\rho$,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:residual}
  s_t^2=\rho s_{t^-}^2+(1-\rho)\min(r_t^2,c^2s_{t^-}^2).
\end{equation}

Clipping prevents a single event from inflating uncertainty for hours.  We use
$\rho=0.94$ and $c=4$ by default.  Fixed-point implementations store $P$ and
$s^2$ in Q16.16; positive diagonal floors prevent collapse after long steady
periods.

\subsection{Risk fusion}

At time $t$, four normalized terms estimate the value of sampling:

\begin{align}
U_t &= \min\!\left(1,\sqrt{P_{xx,t}}/\sigma_U\right), &
S_t &= \min\!\left(1,|r_{t^-}|/(c s_{t^-})\right),\\
A_t &= \min\!\left(1,(t-t_{\mathrm{last}})/\tau_{\max}\right), &
D_t &= q_t\exp(-a_t/\tau_n).
\end{align}

$U_t$ measures forecast uncertainty, $S_t$ retains recent surprise, and $A_t$
prevents starvation.  $D_t$ is a redundancy discount: $q_t$ is the similarity
to the freshest neighbor digest and $a_t$ its age.  Missing or stale digests set
$D_t=0$, making coordination fail open.  The fused risk is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:risk}
u_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
 \left(w_U U_t+w_S S_t+w_A A_t-w_D D_t\right).
\end{equation}

The weights sum to one before the discount.  We use
$w_U=.42$, $w_S=.28$, $w_A=.30$, and $w_D=.20$.  These defaults were selected
on a workload disjoint from evaluation and are held fixed across traces.
Unlike a threshold controller,
the continuous score avoids oscillating between two rates.

\subsection{From risk to an interval}

The unconstrained interval decays exponentially with risk:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interval}
\tau_t^{\star}=\tau_{\min}+
(\tau_{\max}-\tau_{\min})\exp(-\kappa u_t),
\end{equation}

with $\kappa=4.6$.  Exponential mapping dedicates most resolution to the
high-risk region, where a small delay matters.  The application specifies
$\tau_{\min}$ from its shortest relevant event and $\tau_{\max}$ from its
maximum tolerable staleness.  We use 2 and 300\,s.

The mapping is intentionally monotone and bounded.  If two histories differ
only in having larger $U_t$, $S_t$, or $A_t$, the one with higher risk cannot be
sampled later.  More importantly, even a wrongly confident model receives an
observation within $\tau_{\max}$.

\subsection{Energy-budget feedback}
\label{sec:budget}

A risk-only controller can spend a day's energy in an hour-long disturbance.
We regulate expenditure with a token bucket $b_t\in[-B_c,B_c]$.  Tokens arrive
at target power $p_b$ and measured or modeled operation energy $e_t$ removes
them:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bucket}
b_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[-B_c,B_c]}
\left(b_{t^-}+p_b\Delta t-e_t\right).
\end{equation}

Negative balance creates a multiplicative slowdown
\[
  g_t=\exp\!\left(\eta\max(0,-b_t/B_c)\right).
\]
Positive balance does not speed the node
beyond the risk-selected rate; it is reserve for future events.  The scheduled
interval is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finalinterval}
\tau_t=\min\!\left(\tau_{\max},
\max\!\left(\tau_{\min},g_t\tau_t^{\star}\right)\right).
\end{equation}

Two overrides protect fidelity.  A detector trigger pins $\tau_t$ to
$\tau_{\min}$ for a confirmation window $W$, while $A_t=1$ forces a sample even
under energy debt.  Thus the budget is a soft horizon constraint, not a reason
to violate application safety bounds.  If available energy is insufficient
even for one sample per $\tau_{\max}$, the node reports infeasibility rather
than silently degrading.

\subsection{Spatial suppression}

Every $K$th report includes a four-byte digest: a 12-bit quantized feature, a
10-bit slope, a 6-bit uncertainty bin, and a 4-bit epoch.  A node compares the
digest with its own prediction and estimates $q_t$ from normalized feature and
slope distance.  It suppresses only routine reports; event onset and recovery
messages are never suppressed.  Digests are piggybacked on existing frames, so
coordination adds no packet when traffic already exists.

This mechanism differs from cluster-head aggregation~\cite{heinzelman2000energy}
and model-driven acquisition~\cite{deshpande2004model}: there is no elected
leader and no shared global model.  A node can reboot, move, or lose all
neighbors and still run the same local policy.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\small
\caption{One \system scheduler update}
\label{alg:scheduler}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{observation $x_t$, elapsed time $\Delta t$, operation energy $e_t$}
\KwOut{next interval $\tau_t$}
Predict $\hat{z}_{t|t^-}$ and $P_{t|t^-}$ over $\Delta t$\;
$r_t\leftarrow x_t-\hat{x}_{t|t^-}$; update $s_t^2$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual}\;
Correct $\hat{z}_t,P_t$ with $x_t$\;
Read freshest neighbor digest; set $D_t\leftarrow0$ if stale\;
Compute $u_t$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:risk}\;
$\tau_t^\star\leftarrow\tau_{\min}+(\tau_{\max}-\tau_{\min})e^{-\kappa u_t}$\;
$b_t\leftarrow\operatorname{clip}(b_{t^-}+p_b\Delta t-e_t)$\;
$\tau_t\leftarrow\operatorname{clip}(e^{\eta\max(0,-b_t/B_c)}\tau_t^\star)$\;
\If{event pending or maximum age reached}{$\tau_t\leftarrow\tau_{\min}$}
Arm wake timer for $\tau_t$ and persist scheduler state\;
\end{algorithm}
