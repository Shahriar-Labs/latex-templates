\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Our evaluation answers four questions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item Does \system reduce energy at matched event recall?
  \item Does its budget hold during event storms and energy droughts?
  \item How much do uncertainty, feedback, and coordination contribute?
  \item Where does the approach fail?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Methodology}

\paragraph{Workloads.}
Table~\ref{tab:workloads} describes five 30-day workloads.  Temp and Humidity
use trace-aligned generators calibrated to the Corex Berkeley lab data
set~\cite{madden2004tinydb}.  Soil follows seasonal and rain transitions
reported by SensorScope~\cite{barrenetxea2008sensorscope}.  Vibration embeds
short transients in colored noise, and Air combines slow occupancy drift with
step-like ventilation events.  Generators fix random seeds and emit a full-rate
reference stream.  This setup tests policies under controlled events; it does
not claim to reproduce the end-to-end accuracy of those deployments.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\textsc{Periodic} uses the fastest fixed period that reaches at least 95\%
event recall on each workload.  \textsc{Residual} halves its interval when
absolute prediction error crosses a threshold and doubles it after four stable
samples.  \textsc{Variance} maps a rolling 16-sample variance to the same
interval bounds.  \textsc{Oracle} samples densely within ground-truth event
windows and at $\tau_{\max}$ otherwise; it is a non-causal lower bound, not a
deployable competitor.  All causal policies use the same event detector,
interval limits, energy table, radio batching, and initial samples.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
An event is recalled if a sampled stream triggers the reference detector before
the event ends plus a 30\,s grace period.  Delay is measured from reference
onset.  Daily energy includes sleep, sensor, MCU, and radio energy.  Reported
values are medians over 20 generator seeds; error bars are 5th--95th
percentiles.  Unless stated otherwise, the target budget is 2.4\,kJ/day,
$B_c=0.2\Energy$, and packets batch up to four observations.

\subsection{Energy--fidelity trade-off}

Figure~\ref{fig:mainresults}a sweeps each policy's aggressiveness across all
workloads.  At 95\% recall, \system uses 1.92\,kJ/day, 54\% less than
\textsc{Periodic}, 26\% less than \textsc{Residual}, and 21\% less than
\textsc{Variance}.  Its gap to the non-causal oracle is 0.36\,kJ/day.  The gain
comes from long steady intervals without committing to them during rising
uncertainty.

At the default operating point, the per-workload reduction over periodic
sampling ranges from 47\% on Vibration to 63\% on Humidity
(Figure~\ref{fig:mainresults}b).  Vibration leaves the least opportunity because
short events force a 2\,s floor both during and after a trigger.  Humidity is
predictable enough to remain near $\tau_{\max}$ for 71\% of replay time.

\subsection{Budget adherence and delay}

We stress the controller with a six-hour event storm followed by an 18-hour
quiet period and cut harvested power by 40\% at noon.  Figure~\ref{fig:budget}
shows that a risk-only variant exceeds the day budget by 31\%; \system incurs a
brief energy debt, gradually stretches routine intervals, and finishes within
2.8\% of budget.  It does not immediately jump to $\tau_{\max}$, which would
turn an energy correction into a fidelity cliff.

Across normal workloads, median detection delay is 5.7\,s and the 99th
percentile is 18.4\,s, compared with 4.1 and 13.2\,s for the matched-recall
periodic baseline.  The extra 5.2\,s in the tail buys a 54\% energy reduction.
During the synthetic drought, 99th-percentile delay rises to 24.7\,s but remains
below the 30\,s grace bound.  No workload violates $\tau_{\max}$.

\subsection{Ablation and overhead}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} removes one component at a time at the default budget.
Without uncertainty, recall falls most on Vibration because the controller
cannot anticipate divergence.  Without surprise, confident bias persists
through Soil drift.  Removing the governor improves recall slightly but breaks
the budget.  Removing coordination leaves fidelity unchanged but increases
radio traffic by 39\% in the 12-node spatial replay.  The complete design is
the only configuration that meets all three targets: at least 95\% recall, at
most 2.4\,kJ/day, and no more than 65 packets/node/day.

The scheduler itself accounts for 0.7\% of node energy at the 2\,s floor and
less than 0.05\% at the median 74\,s interval.  Its flash image is 6.8\,KiB,
RAM state is 184 bytes, and the exponential table is 128 bytes.  A digest adds
four bytes every $K=8$ routine reports.  Even if no report is suppressed, this
increases radio energy by 2.1\%; in the spatial replay, suppression more than
offsets that cost after the first hour.

\subsection{Sensitivity and robustness}

We vary each default parameter while holding the rest fixed.  For
$\rho\in[0.88,0.98]$, recall changes by at most 1.6 percentage points and energy
by 7.3\%.  Increasing $\kappa$ from 3 to 7 moves the operating point along the
Pareto frontier rather than causing instability.  Bucket capacities below
0.05$\Energy$ react to individual events and add 11\% interval churn; capacities
above 0.5$\Energy$ respond too slowly to the noon harvest cut.  The chosen
0.2$\Energy$ is not universally optimal, but every value from 0.12 to
0.31$\Energy$ stays within 5\% of the default energy--delay product.

We independently drop 0--60\% of neighbor digests.  Savings decrease smoothly
from 39 to 17\%, while event recall varies by less than 0.3 points because
missing digests remove the redundancy discount.  Clock offsets up to one
digest epoch have the same fail-open behavior.  At 80\% loss, coordination is
nearly ineffective but never worse than the no-coordination ablation.
