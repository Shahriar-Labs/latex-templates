\section{Related Work}
Traditional approaches to forest navigation rely heavily on grid-based representations or reactive control schemes. In \cite{nimbus2021octomap}, the authors show that volumetric grids scale poorly as the mapped area expands, leading to significant memory overhead. While octree-based compression mitigates this issue, the underlying representation remains metric rather than topological, which complicates long-range planning under uncertainty.

In contrast, topological map representations simplify path-finding by abstracting free space into networks of nodes and edges \cite{takahashi2022topological}. Recent advancements in solid-state LiDAR technologies \cite{meridian2022lidar} have enabled long-range, high-frequency sensing at a fraction of the weight of rotating scanners. This work builds upon the integration methodologies developed by Miller and Chen \cite{miller2023nexa} for the Synapse-V3 platform, extending the sensing model to support active, non-holonomic topological planning.
