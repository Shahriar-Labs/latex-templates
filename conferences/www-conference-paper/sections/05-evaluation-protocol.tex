\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Worked Benchmark Construction}

The worked example uses a hypothetical collection, \textsc{ChronosQA}, solely
to demonstrate reporting structure.  Table~\ref{tab:data} defines 6,000
questions across four volatility strata.  Each question includes a timestamp,
a concise reference answer, atomic reference claims, eligible and future
evidence, and an answerability label.  Pages are canonicalized, but all
versions are retained.  Annotators judge claim entailment, source independence,
and whether evidence available at $t_q$ supports a unique answer.

Splits are chronological rather than random.  All training questions precede
development questions, which precede test questions.  The retrieval index for
each question is reconstructed as of $t_q$.  Pages from the same canonical URL
or duplicate cluster remain within one split to reduce leakage.  Annotation
guidelines require two independent judgments; disagreements are adjudicated by
a third annotator.  A real study should report agreement, compensation,
demographics relevant to the task, and institutional review requirements.

\subsection{Baselines}

The example compares five configurations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Closed-book} answers from parametric memory without retrieval;
  \item \textbf{BM25+Gen} retrieves lexical matches and generates without
    explicit citation supervision;
  \item \textbf{Dense RAG} uses dense retrieval and a standard
    retrieval-augmented generator \cite{lewis2020rag};
  \item \textbf{Web agent} iteratively searches and cites pages, following the
    browser-assisted paradigm \cite{nakano2021webgpt}; and
  \item \textbf{Chronos} applies temporal filtering, evidence graphs, and
    attribution-constrained generation.
\end{itemize}

All retrieval systems receive the same snapshot.  Generator size, maximum
evidence tokens, decoding parameters, and query budget are matched where
possible.  Hyperparameters are selected once on the development period.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Analysis}

Answer correctness (Ans.) is the macro average of token F1 and exact match.
Citation completeness (Comp.) is the fraction of verifiable output claims with
at least one citation.  Citation precision (Prec.) is the fraction of citations
whose passages entail the associated claim.  Temporal validity (Temp.) is the
fraction of citations eligible under Eq.~\eqref{eq:eligibility}.  Selective risk
is the error rate among answered questions at a fixed coverage.

The primary utility score is declared before evaluation:
\begin{equation}
 U = 0.40\,\mathrm{Ans.}+0.20\,\mathrm{Comp.}
   +0.20\,\mathrm{Prec.}+0.20\,\mathrm{Temp.}
 \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
with every component scaled to $[0,100]$.  We report paired bootstrap 95\%
confidence intervals over questions and apply Holm correction when comparing
multiple systems.  Component metrics remain primary evidence because a weighted
aggregate can hide qualitatively different failures.
