\section{Chronos}
\label{sec:method}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the proposed pipeline.  Its interfaces are
model-agnostic: each component can be replaced without changing the temporal
split or evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Temporal Retrieval}

Chronos first removes documents that violate Eq.~\eqref{eq:eligibility}.  A
hybrid retriever combines lexical score $b_i$, dense similarity $r_i$, and a
freshness prior.  For decay rate $\lambda_q$ determined by the question type,
the freshness of document $d_i$ is
\begin{equation}
  f_i(q)=\exp\!\left[-\lambda_q
  \max\left(0,t_q-t_i^{\mathrm{avail}}\right)\right].
  \label{eq:freshness}
\end{equation}
The retrieval score is
\begin{equation}
  s_i^{\mathrm{ret}} = \alpha b_i + \beta r_i + \gamma f_i(q).
  \label{eq:retrieval}
\end{equation}
Stable questions use $\lambda_q\approx0$; rapidly changing questions use a
larger value.  The coefficients are tuned only on the development period.

\subsection{Evidence Graph Construction}

For the top $K$ passages, Chronos forms a typed graph
$G_q=(V_q,E_q)$.  Each node stores passage text, source domain, timestamps,
retrieval scores, and source type.  Edges take one of four labels:
\emph{supports}, \emph{contradicts}, \emph{derived-from}, or
\emph{near-duplicate}.  A lightweight pairwise classifier proposes semantic
edges; URL canonicalization, content hashes, and outbound links propose
provenance edges.  Thresholds are fixed on development data.

Let $z_i^{(0)}$ be the representation of passage $i$.  A relation-aware update
aggregates neighboring evidence:
\begin{equation}
 z_i^{(\ell+1)} = \sigma\!\left(W_0^{(\ell)}z_i^{(\ell)}+
 \sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{|N_r(i)|}
 \sum_{j\in N_r(i)}W_r^{(\ell)}z_j^{(\ell)}\right).
 \label{eq:message}
\end{equation}
An empty neighborhood contributes zero.  Near-duplicate clusters are pooled
before selection, preventing a copied claim from receiving multiple votes.

\subsection{Evidence Selection and Claim Planning}

The selector assigns passage $i$ the score
\begin{equation}
 s_i^{\mathrm{sel}} = u^\top z_i^{(L)}
 + \eta s_i^{\mathrm{ret}} + \rho a_i - \kappa c_i,
 \label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ estimates source authority and $c_i$ measures contradiction with
higher-authority evidence.  A greedy budgeted selector retains at most $B$
tokens while encouraging domain diversity.  A planner then predicts atomic
claims and links each planned claim to selected passages.  If the best support
score is below threshold $\tau$, the planner marks the claim unsupported; if a
required claim is unsupported, the system abstains.

\subsection{Attribution-Constrained Generation}

The generator receives the question, question time, planned claims, and passage
identifiers.  Training minimizes
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ans}}
 + \mu\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cite}}
 + \nu\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{time}}
 + \xi\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{abstain}},
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
combining answer likelihood, correct claim--citation alignment, penalties for
future evidence, and calibrated abstention.  At inference time, citations are
emitted immediately after the claims they support.  Unsupported sentences are
removed rather than presented without evidence.
