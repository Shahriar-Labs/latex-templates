\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Visual models deployed in the wild encounter blur, weather, sensor changes,
and evolving scene statistics.  Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a model
using unlabelled deployment data and can recover substantial accuracy under
shift~\citep{wang2021tent,niu2022efficient}.  Yet most methods process and
update every patch uniformly.  This is a poor match for many real scenes: rain
may obscure only the upper image, a reflection may occupy one window, and
large background regions may remain stable across adjacent video frames.

Token pruning makes transformer inference cheaper~\citep{rao2021dynamicvit,
bolya2023tome}, but pruning alone does not decide where adaptation is useful.
Conversely, current TTA objectives determine \emph{how} to update a model, not
\emph{which} spatial evidence deserves the computation.  We connect these
ideas with a small router that selects a compute path for every token.  The
result, \method, concentrates adaptation on uncertain or novel regions while
preserving reliable representations.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate test-time token routing as budgeted three-way assignment
        among reuse, adapter, and full-transformer paths.
  \item We combine calibrated entropy, view consistency, and temporal novelty
        in a stable routing score that requires no target labels.
  \item We report accuracy--efficiency trade-offs, controlled ablations, and
        failure cases in a reproducible camera-ready presentation.
\end{itemize}
