\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}
We evaluate on \textbf{MeridianVOS}, a 480-video benchmark with an average of 74 frames per clip and dense multi-object annotations, and \textbf{ApexTrack-400}, a 400-video benchmark emphasizing fast non-rigid motion and frequent occlusion. Both follow the standard semi-supervised protocol: the first-frame mask is given, and the model predicts masks for every subsequent frame. We report mean Jaccard index $\mathcal{J}$, boundary F-measure $\mathcal{F}$, and their average $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$, alongside inference frame rate on a single accelerator at 480p.

\subsection{Baselines and Implementation}
We compare against three dense-attention memory baselines re-implemented under an identical training schedule: a lightweight space-time correspondence network (\emph{STCN-lite}, following~\cite{castillo2022stcn}), a compacted memory transformer (\emph{XMem-S}, following~\cite{adebayo2024xmem}), and a tracking-associated transformer (\emph{AOT-T}, following~\cite{nathan2024aot}). All models, including \methodname{}, use the same backbone and are trained for 120k iterations with a batch size of 16, an initial learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$ decayed with a cosine schedule, and the same augmentation pipeline (random affine, color jitter, and cutout). \methodname{} uses embedding dimension $d=128$, retrieval budget $k=32$, and $\lambda=0.1$ unless stated otherwise.
