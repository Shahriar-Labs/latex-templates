\section{Overview}

This guide converts three class periods on stoichiometry into a flipped
sequence: students meet the vocabulary and worked examples at home, then
spend class time doing the part that actually needs a teacher in the room
--- solving problems, getting stuck, and getting unstuck. The approach
follows the model popularised by \citet{bergmann2012flip} and is
supported by the active-learning evidence synthesised in
\citet{freeman2014active}, which found a 12\% average exam-score gain
when lecture time is replaced with structured practice.

\begin{objectivebox}
By the end of this unit, students will be able to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item Convert between mass, moles, and number of particles using molar mass.
  \item Balance chemical equations and identify mole ratios from coefficients.
  \item Calculate the mass or moles of a product formed from a given mass of reactant (mole-to-mole and mass-to-mass stoichiometry).
  \item Identify a limiting reactant in a two-reactant system and justify the choice in writing.
\end{enumerate}
\end{objectivebox}

\subsection*{The Flipped Cycle}

Figure~\ref{fig:cycle} shows how the three phases of this guide fit
together. Each phase has a distinct job: the pre-class phase transfers
information, the in-class phase builds skill through feedback, and the
post-class phase checks whether the skill transferred.

\begin{teachertip}
If more than a third of the class arrives without having done the
pre-class reading, do not lecture to fill the gap --- run the 5-minute
warm-up quiz in Section~\ref{sec:inclass} anyway, let students self-score,
and redirect unprepared students to the reading during station 1 while
prepared students start station 2. Protects class time for the students
who did the work.
\end{teachertip}
