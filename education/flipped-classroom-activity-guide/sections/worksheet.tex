\section{Station Worksheets}
\label{sec:worksheet}

Each station card below is printed on cardstock, one per group, with
space for whiteboard work rather than answers written on the card
itself. The reaction used throughout is ammonia synthesis, so students
see the same chemistry get harder at each stop.

\subsection*{Station A --- Mole Conversions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=A\arabic*.]
  \item How many moles are in 54.0 g of water ($M = 18.02$ g/mol)?
  \item How many particles are in 0.25 mol of carbon dioxide?
  \item A sample contains $1.505 \times 10^{23}$ particles of glucose. How many moles is this?
  \item Convert 3.0 mol of sodium chloride to grams ($M = 58.44$ g/mol).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Station B --- Mole Ratios}
\begin{enumerate}[label=B\arabic*.]
  \item Balance: \ $\mathrm{\_\,C_3H_8 + \_\,O_2 \rightarrow \_\,CO_2 + \_\,H_2O}$
  \item From the ammonia equation above, how many moles of $\mathrm{H_2}$ are needed for 5 mol of $\mathrm{N_2}$?
  \item How many moles of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ form from 9 mol of $\mathrm{H_2}$, assuming excess $\mathrm{N_2}$?
  \item Write the mole ratio of $\mathrm{O_2}$ to $\mathrm{CO_2}$ for the balanced equation in B1.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Station C --- Mass-to-Mass Stoichiometry}
\begin{enumerate}[label=C\arabic*.]
  \item Nexa Labs reacts 40.0 g of $\mathrm{N_2}$ with excess $\mathrm{H_2}$. What mass of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ forms?
  \item Show all four conversion steps (grams $\to$ moles $\to$ mole ratio $\to$ moles $\to$ grams) for problem C1.
  \item If 68.0 g of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ is required, what minimum mass of $\mathrm{H_2}$ is needed?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Station D --- Limiting Reactant}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=nimbusamber!10, colframe=nimbusamber, boxrule=0.5pt, arc=2pt, left=6pt, right=6pt, top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
\textbf{Scenario:} A Nimbus Manufacturing technician mixes 30.0 g of
$\mathrm{N_2}$ with 8.0 g of $\mathrm{H_2}$ in a sealed reactor.
\end{tcolorbox}
\begin{enumerate}[label=D\arabic*.]
  \item Convert both starting masses to moles.
  \item Using the mole ratio, determine which reactant runs out first.
  \item Calculate the mass of $\mathrm{NH_3}$ actually produced.
  \item In one sentence, explain to a classmate why you can't just use the reactant with the smaller mass to decide what's limiting.
\end{enumerate}
