\section{Scope and Context}

\subsection{Business context}

Asteria processes merchant catalogs, quotes, orders, and payment-provider tokens for customers in North America and Europe. It has a predictable daily baseline with campaign-driven bursts up to five times normal traffic. The platform must deploy frequently without compromising transaction integrity or auditability.

\subsection{In scope}

\begin{itemize}
  \item AWS organization, accounts, identity federation, guardrails, and centralized audit logging.
  \item DNS, edge protection, regional networking, compute, data services, and service-to-service connectivity.
  \item Continuous delivery, infrastructure as code, secrets, observability, backup, recovery, and cost controls.
  \item Production and structurally equivalent non-production environments.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out of scope}

Application-domain design, payment-provider implementation, end-user device security, corporate office networking, detailed data-retention schedules, and business continuity outside the technology platform are governed by separate documents.

\subsection{Architecture principles}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Design for failure.} Components are replaceable; recovery paths are automated and exercised.
  \item \textbf{Minimize trust.} Authenticate every workload, authorize the narrow action, and record the decision.
  \item \textbf{Keep data authoritative.} A single system owns each mutable record; integration is event-driven where practical.
  \item \textbf{Automate repeatable change.} Consoles are diagnostic tools, not a deployment mechanism.
  \item \textbf{Expose operational truth.} Every service publishes health, golden signals, ownership, and runbook links.
  \item \textbf{Cost is a design dimension.} Capacity, retention, and service tiers have explicit value hypotheses.
\end{enumerate}
