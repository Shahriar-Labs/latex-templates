\section{Conclusion}

\begin{frame}{Summary of Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item AQR reduces p95 latency by \textbf{34--46\%} across three datasets versus the strongest single-index or heuristic baseline, at equal or higher recall.
\item Query-level features generalize: a router trained on SIFT1M transfers to Meridian-Corpus-5M with only a 2.1-point recall drop, no retraining.
\item Routing overhead ($\approx$1.1\,ms) is more than an order of magnitude smaller than the latency it saves.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.3cm}
\textbf{Limitations.} The cost model retrains offline every 6 hours and does not yet react to intra-window drift. We evaluated read-heavy workloads only --- write-heavy / streaming ingestion is future work.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Future Work}
\begin{itemize}
\item Online cost-model updates via bandit feedback instead of periodic offline retraining \cite{ramirez2021bandit}.
\item Extending the router to write-heavy and streaming-ingestion workloads.
\item Multi-tenant fairness: preventing one tenant's routing decisions from starving another's queue.
\item Deploying AQR as a sidecar in front of production systems such as Nexa-VDB and Vertex Store.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
