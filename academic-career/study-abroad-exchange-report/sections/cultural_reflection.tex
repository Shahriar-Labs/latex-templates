\section{Cultural Reflection and Social Life}
\label{sec:cultural_reflection}

Living in Zurich offered a fascinating glimpse into Swiss culture, marked by high efficiency, public order, and an appreciation for the outdoors. While Switzerland's high cost of living presented an initial shock, careful budgeting and utilizing student discounts allowed me to fully engage with the local lifestyle.

\subsection{Living in Zurich}
Zurich is a bilingual city in practice; while High German is used in formal writing and administration, Swiss German (\emph{Schwiizerdütsch}) is the spoken dialect of daily life. The pre-semester language course was crucial for learning local greetings and basic expressions, which Swiss people highly appreciated when I attempted to use them.

The efficiency of Zurich's public transportation system is unparalleled. Utilizing a network of trams, trains, and ferries, I could travel from my suburban dormitory to the city center or deep into the mountains with ease. The university subsidized a regional transit pass, which encouraged regular exploration of the canton and surrounding areas.

\subsection{Excursions and Travel}
One of the highlights of my exchange was exploring the natural landscape of Switzerland. Over weekends, group trips with fellow students took us to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Lucerne and Mount Pilatus:} A classic introduction to Central Switzerland, combining historic covered bridges with steep mountain ascents.
  \item \textbf{Grindelwald:} A weekend hiking in the shadow of the Eiger and Jungfrau peaks, showing the scale of the Swiss Alps.
  \item \textbf{The Rhine Falls:} Located near the northern border, showcasing the powerful natural hydrology of Europe's largest waterfall.
\end{itemize}

These travels, supported in part by the Nimbus Mobility Grant \cite{nimbus2025report}, enriched my understanding of European history and geography while fostering lasting friendships with students from diverse backgrounds.
