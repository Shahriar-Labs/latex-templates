Table~\ref{tab:gain} confirms a mid-band gain magnitude of $9.82$ ---
within \qty{1.8}{\percent} of the theoretical value $10.0$.  Gain rolls
off above \qty{10}{\kHz} because of the LM741's finite gain-bandwidth
product (GBWP $\approx\qty{1}{\MHz}$).

\subsection{Active Low-Pass Filter \textemdash\ Frequency Response}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Sallen--Key LPF: frequency response data (excerpt of 10 points).}
  \label{tab:lpf}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}S[table-format=6.0] S[table-format=1.3] S[table-format=1.4] S[table-format=-2.2]@{}}
    \toprule
    \multicolumn{1}{c}{\textbf{Frequency (Hz)}} &

    \bottomrule

\end{table}

The measured \qty{-3}{\dB} frequency is \qty{1.02}{\kHz} (theoretical:
\qty{1.07}{\kHz}, error \qty{4.7}{\percent}).  The roll-off between
\qty{2}{\kHz} and \qty{20}{\kHz} is \qty{-39.8}{\dB/decade}, confirming
the expected second-order Butterworth slope.

\begin{resultbox}
  \textbf{Key Finding:} The Sallen--Key filter achieves
  \qty{-60}{\dB} stop-band attenuation at \qty{100}{\kHz}, adequate
  for anti-aliasing in a \qty{10}{\kHz}-bandwidth data-acquisition system.
\end{resultbox}
