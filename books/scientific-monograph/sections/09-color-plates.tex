\begin{colorplate}{accent}
{\bfseries Power-law degree distributions.} Empirical degree distributions for three real-world networks, plotted on double-logarithmic axes. Left: the Internet at the autonomous-system level ($N \approx 25{,}000$, estimated exponent $\gamma = 2.1$). Center: a co-authorship network in condensed-matter physics ($N \approx 40{,}000$, $\gamma = 2.4$). Right: word co-occurrence in \textit{Moby Dick} ($N \approx 17{,}000$, $\gamma = 2.8$). The dashed lines show maximum-likelihood power-law fits; shaded regions indicate 95\% confidence intervals obtained via parametric bootstrap with $B = 10{,}000$ resamples.
\end{colorplate}

\bigskip

\begin{colorplate}{accentwarm}
{\bfseries Phase diagram of the correlated Erd\H{o}s--R\'{e}nyi ensemble.} The axes represent the average degree $c$ and the assortativity strength $\alpha$. Region I (blue): subcritical regime with no giant component and exponentially decaying component-size distribution. Region II (green): a single giant component spanning approximately $m(c) \cdot N$ vertices. Region III (orange): a \textit{bimodal} phase in which two giant components coexist, stabilized by strong degree--degree correlations. The critical line separating Regions I and II is second-order; the transition between II and III is first-order. The tricritical point at $(c, \alpha) \approx (2.4, 0.62)$ marks the meeting of the two phase boundaries.
\end{colorplate}

\bigskip

\begin{colorplate}{accentgreen}
{\bfseries Community detection via belief propagation.} Application of the degree-corrected stochastic block model to a friendship network of $N = 1{,}034$ students at a U.S.\ high school. Left panel: the adjacency matrix reordered according to the inferred block assignment, revealing five distinct communities that correspond closely to self-reported social groups. Right panel: the same matrix randomized while preserving the degree sequence, demonstrating that the block structure is statistically significant ($p < 10^{-4}$ under a parametric bootstrap test). Colors indicate edge density within and between blocks, normalized by the expected density under the configuration-model null.
\end{colorplate}
