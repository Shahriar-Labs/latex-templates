Pruned neural networks keep most of their accuracy with a small fraction of their weights, but
dense accelerators cannot turn that sparsity into speed. This dissertation designs hardware that
does. It characterizes the sparsity patterns of pruned convolutional and transformer models,
proposes a processing element that skips zero operands in both weights and activations, and
evaluates the resulting accelerator in a cycle-accurate simulator. At 90\% weight sparsity the
design runs 3.4 times faster than a dense baseline of equal area and uses 2.7 times less energy
per inference.
