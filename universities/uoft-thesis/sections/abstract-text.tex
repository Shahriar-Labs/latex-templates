Labelled images are expensive, and the representations learned from them transfer poorly to
tasks their labels did not anticipate. Self-supervised learning replaces labels with a task the
data poses to itself, such as matching two augmented views of one image. This thesis studies
which choices in that task decide the quality of the learned representation. Chapter~2 reviews
contrastive, distillation and masked-prediction methods. Chapter~3 introduces a view-selection
scheme that chooses crops by predicted overlap in content rather than at random. Chapter~4
evaluates it on linear probing, low-shot classification and dense prediction, where it improves
ten-shot accuracy by 3.2 points over the strongest random-crop baseline at equal compute.
