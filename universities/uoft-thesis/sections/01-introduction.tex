\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Deep networks trained on large labelled datasets learn features that transfer surprisingly well,
but the labels themselves limit what the features encode. Self-supervised methods learn from
unlabelled images by solving a pretext task, and at scale they now match or exceed supervised
pre-training on many downstream benchmarks \citep{chen2020simclr, he2020moco}.

Most of these methods agree on the broad recipe: create two views of an image, encode both, and
train the encoder so the views agree. They disagree on the details, and the details matter.
This thesis isolates one of them, how the two views are chosen, and shows that choosing views
by their content rather than at random yields better representations for the same compute.
