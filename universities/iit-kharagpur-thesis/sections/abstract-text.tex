Distribution grids with high shares of rooftop solar must balance generation and demand in real
time, and conventional optimization struggles when forecasts are uncertain. This thesis applies
reinforcement learning to two grid-control problems: volt-var control with smart inverters and
scheduling of community battery storage. It formulates both as Markov decision processes,
trains policies with a safety layer that projects actions onto the feasible set of the power
flow constraints, and evaluates them on a modified 33-bus test feeder. The learned volt-var
policy keeps all bus voltages within limits in 99.6\% of simulated hours while reducing network
losses by 11\% compared with a droop-control baseline.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} reinforcement learning, smart grid, volt-var control,
battery scheduling, safe learning
