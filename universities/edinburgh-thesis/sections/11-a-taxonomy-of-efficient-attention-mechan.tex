\section{A Taxonomy of Efficient Attention Mechanisms}
\label{sec:background-taxonomy}

The quadratic complexity of standard self-attention has motivated a large body of work on
efficient approximations. Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} provides a structured overview of the
design space.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{A taxonomy of efficient attention mechanisms categorised by sparsity structure,
    context dependence, and use of external memory.}
  \label{tab:taxonomy}
  \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\color{edinburghblue}\bfseries}l@{\hspace{8mm}}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Category} & \textbf{Mechanism} & \textbf{Key Works} \\
    \midrule
    Fixed Sparse
      & Predefined sparsity patterns (local windows, strided, dilated)
      & \cite{child2019generating, beltagy2020longformer} \\
    Learned Sparse
      & Input-dependent sparsity via hashing, clustering, or routing
      & \cite{kitaev2020reformer, roy2021efficient} \\
    Low-Rank
      & Kernelised or linearised attention via feature maps
      & \cite{katharopoulos2020transformers, choromanski2021rethinking} \\
    Memory-Augmented
      & External memory with read/write operations; compression
      & \cite{rae2019compressive, dai2019transformer} \\
    Recurrence-Based
      & Recurrent connection across transformer layers
      & \cite{dai2019transformer} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Each category trades off a different dimension of the accuracy--efficiency Pareto frontier.
Fixed-sparse methods offer predictable speedups but may miss critical long-range
connections; learned-sparse methods adapt to the input but incur routing overhead;
low-rank methods achieve linear complexity but can struggle with tasks requiring precise
token-level interactions. The work presented in this thesis spans the learned-sparse
(Chapter~\ref{chap:sca}) and memory-augmented (Chapter~\ref{chap:hmg}) categories.
