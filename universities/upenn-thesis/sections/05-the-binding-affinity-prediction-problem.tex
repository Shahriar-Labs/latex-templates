\section{The Binding Affinity Prediction Problem}

Formally, the binding affinity prediction problem can be stated as follows: given a protein structure $\mathcal{P}$ and a ligand structure $\mathcal{L}$, predict the equilibrium dissociation constant $K_d$ or the binding free energy $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$, related by the thermodynamic relationship:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:thermo}
\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = -RT \ln K_d = \Delta H_{\text{bind}} - T\Delta S_{\text{bind}}
\end{equation}

where $R$ is the gas constant, $T$ is the absolute temperature, $\Delta H_{\text{bind}}$ is the binding enthalpy, and $\Delta S_{\text{bind}}$ is the binding entropy. The challenge lies in the fact that $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ is typically on the order of $-5$ to $-15$ kcal/mol, representing a small difference between large opposing energetic contributions \cite{Mobley2010}.
