\section{Convex Risk Measures}

Value-at-Risk (VaR) --- the $\alpha$-quantile of the loss distribution --- became the regulatory standard through the Basel Accords. Its theoretical deficiencies are well documented: VaR is not a coherent risk measure~\cite{artzner1999} because it fails the sub-additivity axiom. Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR), defined as the expected loss exceeding VaR,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{CVaR}_\alpha(L) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[L \mid L \geq \operatorname{VaR}_\alpha(L)\right],
\label{eq:cvar}
\end{equation}
is coherent and convex~\cite{rockafellar2000}. Rockafellar and Uryasev~\cite{rockafellar2002} showed that CVaR minimization can be cast as a linear program when the return distribution is represented by scenarios --- an insight we exploit in Chapter~4, extending it to mixture-of-Gaussians representations.

\begin{definitionbox}
\textbf{Definition 2 (Coherent Risk Measure).} A risk measure $\rho: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ is \textit{coherent} if it satisfies (i)~monotonicity: $X \leq Y \Rightarrow \rho(X) \geq \rho(Y)$, (ii)~sub-additivity: $\rho(X+Y) \leq \rho(X) + \rho(Y)$, (iii)~positive homogeneity: $\rho(\lambda X) = \lambda\rho(X)$ for $\lambda \geq 0$, and (iv)~translational invariance: $\rho(X + c) = \rho(X) - c$.
\end{definitionbox}
