\section{Background and Motivation}
The proliferation of latency-sensitive applications -- augmented-reality retail experiences, autonomous inspection drones, and real-time logistics tracking -- has pushed computation away from centralised data centres and toward the network edge. Figure~\ref{fig:continuum} illustrates the resulting three-tier \emph{edge-cloud continuum}: constrained device-tier sensors, latency-favourable edge clusters positioned near points of presence, and elastic cloud regions offering near-unbounded capacity at higher round-trip cost.

Scheduling decisions in this continuum must jointly reason about compute placement, network latency, and monetary cost, under workload patterns that shift on the order of seconds. Static or single-objective schedulers -- common in current commercial offerings including Vertex CloudOps and Apex Fabric -- either under-utilise edge capacity during quiet periods or violate service-level agreements (SLAs) during bursts.
