Superconducting qubits are among the leading platforms for quantum computing, but their errors
occur far too often for long computations. Quantum error correction protects information by
encoding it redundantly and decoding the resulting error syndromes faster than errors accumulate.
This thesis studies decoding for the surface code on superconducting hardware. It proposes a
decoder that combines minimum-weight perfect matching with a learned correction for correlated
errors caused by leakage and crosstalk, and an implementation that meets the one-microsecond
syndrome cycle of current devices. On simulated noise calibrated to device measurements, the
decoder lowers the logical error rate of a distance-seven code by 38\% compared with standard
matching.
