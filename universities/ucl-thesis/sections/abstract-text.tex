Medical imaging datasets are small, unevenly labelled and hard to share. Generative models
that synthesize realistic images could augment training sets and translate between imaging
modalities, but only if the synthetic images preserve the anatomy that clinical decisions rely
on. This thesis develops and evaluates generative models for magnetic resonance imaging with
that requirement in mind. It proposes a diffusion model conditioned on segmentation maps, so
that synthetic anatomy can be specified rather than hoped for, and introduces an evaluation
protocol that measures anatomical fidelity with a segmentation network rather than with image
statistics alone. Augmenting a small training set with conditioned synthetic images raises the
Dice score of a downstream tumour segmentation model from 0.71 to 0.78.
