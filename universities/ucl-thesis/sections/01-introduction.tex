\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Deep learning has transformed medical image analysis, but it needs labelled data that medicine
struggles to provide. Generative models offer a partial remedy. Adversarial networks
\citep{goodfellow2014} and, more recently, denoising diffusion models \citep{ho2020} can
synthesize images that are hard to tell from real ones.

Looking real is not enough in medicine. A synthetic brain scan with a plausible but wrong
ventricle shape could teach a model the wrong anatomy. This thesis therefore conditions
generation on anatomy and evaluates it on anatomy.
