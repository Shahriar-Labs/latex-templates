\section{Baseline Comparison}
Three charging strategies are compared over a representative summer weekday: uncoordinated
(charge-on-arrival), rule-based staggering, and the proposed MPC scheduler. Table~\ref{tab:results}
summarizes performance across the three evaluation metrics.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Performance comparison across charging strategies}
\label{tab:results}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Uncoordinated} & \textbf{Rule-Based} & \textbf{MPC (Proposed)} \\
\midrule
Peak transformer loading (\%) & 118.6 & 102.3 & \textbf{86.1} \\
Worst-case voltage dev.\ (\%) & 6.9 & 5.1 & \textbf{4.0} \\
Energy cost (SAR/day) & 1{,}842 & 1{,}705 & \textbf{1{,}610} \\
Departure SoC target met (\%) & 94.1 & 96.4 & \textbf{98.7} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Relative to the uncoordinated baseline, the MPC scheduler reduces peak transformer loading by
27.4\% and improves worst-case voltage deviation by 41.8\%, while simultaneously lowering
energy cost by 12.6\% and improving SoC target satisfaction. This confirms that thermal,
voltage, and cost objectives can be jointly improved rather than traded off, primarily because
the receding-horizon formulation shifts flexible load away from the evening peak without
delaying vehicles past their required departure times.
