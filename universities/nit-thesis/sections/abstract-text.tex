Fibre-reinforced polymer composites are designed by choosing fibres, matrix, layup and
processing conditions, and each candidate is traditionally tested by expensive experiments or
finite-element simulation. This thesis builds machine-learning surrogates that predict the
stiffness and strength of laminated composites from their design variables, and uses them to
search the design space. A Gaussian-process surrogate trained on 600 finite-element simulations
predicts laminate stiffness within 3\% and guides a Bayesian optimization that finds layups 18\%
lighter than a baseline design at equal stiffness, confirmed by three physical tests.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} composite materials, machine learning, Gaussian processes,
Bayesian optimization, laminate design
