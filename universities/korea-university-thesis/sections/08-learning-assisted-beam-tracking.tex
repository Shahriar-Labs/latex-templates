\section{Learning-Assisted Beam Tracking}
Recent work has explored using channel history, inertial sensors, or vision
data to predict beam direction ahead of a sweep. These methods reduce average
search time but are typically evaluated in simulation or controlled indoor
settings, leaving open questions about robustness to the irregular blockage
patterns of dense outdoor deployments. Table~\ref{tab:related} summarizes
representative approaches along the dimensions most relevant to this thesis.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Comparison of representative beam management approaches.}
\label{tab:related}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXcc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Approach} & \textbf{Prediction Signal} & \textbf{Outdoor Eval.} & \textbf{Overhead} \\
\midrule
Hierarchical codebook search & None (exhaustive) & Yes & Low \\
Kalman-filter beam tracking & Channel history & Limited & Low \\
Vision-assisted prediction & Camera feed & No & High \\
Inertial-aided prediction & IMU + channel state & Limited & Medium \\
\rowcolor{kupale} \textsc{Meridian-Beam} (proposed) & Channel state + RL policy & Yes (field) & Medium \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
