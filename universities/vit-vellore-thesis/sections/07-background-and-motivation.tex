\section{Background and Motivation}
Unplanned downtime remains one of the largest controllable cost drivers on a modern
factory floor. Industry surveys commonly attribute losses of several percent of annual
revenue to unscheduled equipment stoppages, a figure that motivates the growing adoption
of \keyword{condition-based monitoring} using vibration, thermal, and electrical-current
sensors attached to rotating machinery. As these sensor fleets have grown from dozens to
thousands of nodes per site, two constraints have become dominant: the cost of hauling
raw, high-frequency telemetry to a central data lake, and the reluctance of plant
operators to expose proprietary process signatures embedded in that telemetry to an
external cloud provider.

\keyword{Federated learning} offers an attractive middle ground. Rather than moving data
to the model, FL moves a lightweight model to the data: each edge gateway trains locally
on its own telemetry and periodically shares only model updates with a coordinating
server, which aggregates them into a global model. This thesis asks whether that
paradigm can match the predictive accuracy of centralised training while respecting the
bandwidth and confidentiality constraints typical of a brownfield industrial deployment.
