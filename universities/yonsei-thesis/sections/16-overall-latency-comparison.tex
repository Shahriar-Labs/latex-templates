\section{Overall Latency Comparison}

ATS was evaluated against round-robin and static-priority baselines over
20 independent trace replays. Table~\ref{tab:results} reports mean and
tail latency alongside sustained cluster utilization.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Latency and utilization results across scheduling strategies (lower latency is better).}
\label{tab:results}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{yonseiblue}
\textcolor{white}{\textbf{Strategy}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Mean Lat.\ (ms)}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{$P_{95}$ Lat.\ (ms)}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Utilization (\%)}}\\
\midrule
Round-robin & 61.2 & 214.8 & 58.3\\
\rowcolor{paletint}
Static priority & 54.7 & 187.1 & 63.9\\
ATS (this work) & \textbf{41.9} & \textbf{132.3} & \textbf{77.7}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Relative to the static-priority baseline, ATS reduces $P_{95}$ latency by
38.4\% (from 187.1\,ms to 132.3\,ms) while raising sustained utilization
by 21.7 percentage points relative to round-robin.
