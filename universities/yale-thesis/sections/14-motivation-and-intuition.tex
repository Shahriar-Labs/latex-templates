\section{Motivation and Intuition}

The adaptive methods surveyed in Chapter~2 differ in their precise update rules, yet they all
share a common template: given a stochastic gradient $g_t$, compute a \emph{preconditioner}
matrix $P_t \succ 0$ (or its diagonal approximation), and update
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta_t\, P_t^{-1} g_t.
  \label{eq:generic_precond}
\end{equation}
For SGD, $P_t = I$; for AdaGrad, $P_t = \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{G_t})$; for Adam,
$P_t = \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\hat{v}_t})$.

The difficulty in analyzing adaptive methods stems from the fact that $P_t$ depends on the
entire history of gradients, introducing complex statistical dependencies between the
preconditioner and the current gradient. Our key insight is that these dependencies can be
bounded through a careful \emph{normalization} argument that decouples the magnitude of the
update from the geometry of the preconditioner.
