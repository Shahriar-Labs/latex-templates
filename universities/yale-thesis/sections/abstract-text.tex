Genomic studies now routinely measure hundreds of thousands of variants on a few thousand
people, so the number of candidate predictors dwarfs the sample size. Classical tests lose their
calibration in this regime, and methods that select variables give no honest statement of
uncertainty about what they select. This dissertation develops inference procedures for sparse
high-dimensional regression that remain valid when predictors are strongly correlated, as
genotypes in linkage disequilibrium are. Chapter~\ref{ch:background} reviews the debiased lasso
and knockoff filters. Chapter~\ref{ch:method} introduces a block-debiased estimator that
treats each linkage block as a unit, and pairs it with a knockoff construction that preserves
the block structure. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} shows in simulation that the procedure holds the
false discovery rate at its nominal level where the unblocked methods exceed it by a factor of
two, and applies it to an expression quantitative trait locus study.
