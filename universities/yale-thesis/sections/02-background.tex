\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

We observe a response $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and a design matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times p}$
with $p \gg n$, and assume the sparse linear model
\begin{equation}
  y = X\beta + \varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_n),
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where only $s \ll n$ entries of $\beta$ are non-zero. The lasso \citep{tibshirani1996}
estimates $\beta$ by penalized least squares, but its coefficients are biased toward zero and
have no simple sampling distribution. Debiased estimators \citep{vandegeer2014,
javanmard2014} add a one-step correction that restores asymptotic normality.

When thousands of hypotheses are tested at once, the relevant error rate is the false
discovery rate, the expected proportion of false discoveries among all discoveries
\citep{benjamini1995}. Knockoff filters \citep{barber2015} control it in finite samples by
constructing synthetic copies of the predictors that mimic their correlation but are known to
be null.
