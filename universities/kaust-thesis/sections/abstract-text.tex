Reverse osmosis produces most of the world's desalinated water, and its energy use is set by
how permeable and how selective its membranes are. Polyamide thin-film composite membranes have
improved slowly, trading permeability against salt rejection. This dissertation investigates
two routes past that trade-off. It incorporates aligned graphene-oxide nanosheets into the
polyamide layer to create fast water channels, and it tunes interfacial polymerization to
produce thinner, crumpled selective layers with more surface area. Membranes combining both
reached a water permeance of 5.1 litres per square metre per hour per bar with 99.6\% sodium
chloride rejection, nearly double the permeance of a commercial seawater membrane tested under
the same conditions.
