\section{Summary of Contributions}

This thesis has developed and validated a suite of Bayesian hierarchical models
for ecological monitoring in southeastern Australian forests. The principal
contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt]
  \item A flexible, multi-species occupancy framework that jointly models
        presence--absence, count, and presence-only data through a shared
        latent ecological process (Chapter~3).
  \item The first application of multivariate Gaussian process spatial random
        effects to Australian forest monitoring data, demonstrating that
        residual spatial structure contains ecologically interpretable
        information about species interactions and unmeasured environmental
        gradients.
  \item A comprehensive simulation study that establishes the conditions under
        which hierarchical Bayesian models offer practical advantages over
        conventional single-species methods, particularly for rare and cryptic
        taxa.
  \item An empirical case study of the Australian Alps avifauna that provides
        new insights into the environmental drivers of occupancy for six
        species of conservation concern, including the endangered Gang-gang
        Cockatoo.
\end{enumerate}
