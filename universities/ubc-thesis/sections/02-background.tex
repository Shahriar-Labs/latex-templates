\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

A Markov decision process is a tuple $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, r, \gamma)$. A policy
$\pi_\theta(a \mid s)$ is trained to maximize the expected discounted return
\begin{equation}
  J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_\theta}\Bigl[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^{t} r(s_t, a_t)\Bigr],
  \label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
here by proximal policy optimization \citep{schulman2017}. For scheduling, the reward at each
step is the negative number of jobs in the system, so maximizing $J$ minimizes average job
completion time by Little's law \citep{sutton2018}.
