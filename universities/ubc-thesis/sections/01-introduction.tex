\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

A cluster scheduler decides, many times per second, which queued job to start and on which
machines. Good decisions shorten how long jobs wait; poor ones leave machines idle while work
queues up. Production schedulers rely on heuristics such as shortest-job-first or dominant
resource fairness, which are simple to reason about but cannot adapt to a particular workload.

Deep reinforcement learning has learned control policies for games \citep{mnih2015} and early
work showed it could learn simple packing policies \citep{mao2016}. This thesis asks whether
it can produce schedulers that are both better than heuristics and safe to deploy.
