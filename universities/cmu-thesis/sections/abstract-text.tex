Large language models answer many questions that require combining facts, yet they fail in
revealing ways when a question combines familiar parts in an unfamiliar arrangement. This
thesis studies that gap. It introduces a benchmark that separates compositional structure from
surface familiarity, shows that step-by-step prompting helps most on shallow compositions and
least on deep ones, and proposes a decomposition procedure that plans sub-questions before
answering them. On compositions of depth four, the procedure raises exact-match accuracy from
31\% to 58\% for a model of fixed size.
