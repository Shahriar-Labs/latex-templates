Two-dimensional crystals can host electronic phases whose defining properties are topological:
they are fixed by integers that survive any smooth deformation of the material. This
dissertation studies how such phases arise in layered transition-metal compounds, and how they
can be identified from transport measurements that are available in practice. Chapter~2
reviews the band-theory description of the quantum spin Hall insulator. Chapter~3 develops a
tight-binding model for a strained monolayer and computes its $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant as a
function of strain and spin-orbit coupling. Chapter~4 compares the predicted phase diagram
with edge-conductance data from four devices and finds a transition near 1.8\% biaxial strain,
where the edge conductance approaches the quantized value $2e^2/h$.
