\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

The integer quantum Hall effect showed that a measured conductance can be quantized with
astonishing precision because it is fixed by a topological invariant of the electronic
bands \citep{thouless1982}. Haldane then showed that no external magnetic field is needed:
a lattice model with broken time-reversal symmetry can realize the same physics
\citep{haldane1988}. Kane and Mele extended the idea to systems that preserve time reversal,
predicting the quantum spin Hall insulator \citep{kane2005}, which was soon observed in HgTe
quantum wells \citep{bernevig2006}.

Layered materials offer a new setting for these ideas, because strain, twist angle and gating
can tune a single monolayer across a topological transition. This dissertation asks two
questions: where in parameter space does the transition occur for a realistic monolayer, and
what signature of it survives in imperfect devices?
