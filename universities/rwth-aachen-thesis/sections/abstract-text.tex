Electric vehicles convert battery energy into motion through an inverter and a permanent-magnet
synchronous machine whose efficiency depends strongly on the operating point. This dissertation
develops a model predictive controller that chooses current references for the machine and the
torque split between two drive units so as to minimize losses while tracking the driver's torque
demand. A convex loss model makes the optimization solvable within the 100-microsecond control
period of the inverter. On a dual-motor test bench driven through a standard urban cycle, the
controller reduces drivetrain losses by 7.8\% compared with a fixed 50/50 torque split.
