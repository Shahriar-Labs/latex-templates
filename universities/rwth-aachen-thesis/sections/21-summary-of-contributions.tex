\section{Summary of Contributions}

This thesis has addressed the gap between the theory and practice of adaptive stochastic
optimization for high-dimensional machine learning. We introduced the Normalized Preconditioned
Gradient (NPG) framework, which provides a unified language for describing and analyzing
adaptive methods. Within this framework, we proved almost-sure convergence to stationary points
under assumptions that are both weaker and more faithful to practice than those in the existing
literature.

Building on the NPG analysis, we proposed \textsc{Nadam}, an algorithm that integrates
Nesterov-style acceleration with adaptive preconditioning. \textsc{Nadam} enjoys an
$\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$ convergence rate for non-convex objectives — strictly tighter than
the best known bound for Adam — and achieves state-of-the-art empirical performance on image
classification, language modeling, and reinforcement learning benchmarks.
