Clinical prediction models improve with data from many hospitals, but privacy law and patient
trust make it difficult to pool records. Federated learning trains a shared model by exchanging
parameter updates instead of data. This dissertation studies federated learning for tabular
clinical data, where hospitals differ in case mix, coding practice and size. It proposes a
reweighting of client updates that accounts for differences in outcome prevalence, and combines
it with differentially private aggregation. Across eleven simulated hospitals, the method
recovers 96\% of the discrimination of a model trained on pooled data while guaranteeing
$(\epsilon, \delta) = (3, 10^{-5})$ differential privacy for every patient.
