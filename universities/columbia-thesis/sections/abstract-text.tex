Large computations now run across thousands of machines, and at that scale some machine is
always failing. This dissertation studies how to keep such computations correct and fast when
failures are routine. It shows that replicating coordination state with a consensus protocol,
while recovering data-parallel work by recomputation from lineage, gives better throughput than
replicating everything, and it presents a scheduler that places recomputation away from
recently failed machines. In experiments on a 512-node cluster with injected failures, the
design sustains 94\% of failure-free throughput at a failure rate at which checkpoint-based
recovery sustains 71\%.
