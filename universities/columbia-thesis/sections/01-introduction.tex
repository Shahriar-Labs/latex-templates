\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Programming models such as MapReduce made it practical to run a computation over thousands of
machines by hiding failures from the programmer \citep{dean2008}. In-memory frameworks later
made iterative computations fast by recovering lost data from its lineage instead of from
replicas \citep{zaharia2012}. Both depend on a small amount of coordination state, such as
which tasks have finished and where their outputs live, that must survive failures exactly.

This dissertation separates the two kinds of state and treats each with the mechanism that
suits it: consensus for coordination, recomputation for data.
