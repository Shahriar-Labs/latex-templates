Proteins inside living cells move by diffusion, binding and active transport, and how they move
reveals how they work. Single-molecule tracking follows individual labelled proteins, but in
the crowded interior of a cell the trajectories are short and noisy. This dissertation combines
light-sheet illumination, which reduces background, with a hidden Markov analysis that
separates freely diffusing from transiently bound states. Applied to a transcription factor in
live human cells, the analysis finds that 34\% of molecules are bound at any time, with a mean
residence time of 1.9 seconds.
