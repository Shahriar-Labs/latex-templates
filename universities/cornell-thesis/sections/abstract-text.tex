Small aerial vehicles must perceive their surroundings with light, low-power sensors while
moving fast, and their failures are expensive. This dissertation studies how onboard perception
degrades under the conditions aerial robots actually meet, namely motion blur, low light and
rapidly changing viewpoint, and how to make it degrade gracefully. Chapter~2 reviews visual
odometry and learned object detection. Chapter~3 proposes a detector that predicts its own
localization uncertainty and a planner that slows the vehicle when that uncertainty grows.
Chapter~4 reports 214 flights in a forested test area, where the combination cut collisions from
nine to one while lowering mean speed by only 12\%.
