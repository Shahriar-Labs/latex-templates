\chapter{Background}
\label{ch:background}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be an undirected graph with $n = |V|$ vertices and $m = |E|$ edges, adjacency
matrix $A$ and degrees $d_i$. The modularity of a partition $\mathcal{C}$ of $V$ is
\begin{equation}
  Q(\mathcal{C}) = \frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i,j}\Bigl(A_{ij} - \frac{d_i d_j}{2m}\Bigr)
  \mathbf{1}[c_i = c_j],
  \label{eq:modularity}
\end{equation}
which compares the number of edges inside communities with the number expected at random
\citep{newman2006}. The Louvain method greedily moves vertices between communities to
increase $Q$ and then contracts the result \citep{blondel2008}. Spectral sparsifiers keep a
small reweighted subset of edges that preserves every cut to within a factor of $1 \pm
\epsilon$ \citep{spielman2004}.
