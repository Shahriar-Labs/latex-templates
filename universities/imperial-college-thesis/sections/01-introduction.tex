\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

Arterial walls are not rigid. Over a cardiac cycle the aorta expands by several per cent in
diameter, storing energy in systole and returning it in diastole. Rigid-wall models therefore
misestimate pressure waves and wall shear stress, the quantities clinicians use to assess the
risk of aneurysm growth and rupture.

Coupled fluid-structure interaction (FSI) simulation resolves the interaction but is costly,
and partitioned solvers suffer an instability when the wall is light relative to the fluid
\citep{causin2005}. This thesis removes that instability at modest cost.
