Blood flow in large arteries deforms the vessel wall, and the wall's motion in turn changes the
flow. Predicting both together is needed to estimate the stresses that drive aneurysm growth,
yet coupled simulations are expensive and often unstable when the wall is light compared with
the blood. This thesis develops a partitioned solver for this regime. Chapter~2 reviews the
arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian formulation and the added-mass instability. Chapter~3 introduces
a Robin-type interface condition with a coefficient estimated from the wall's local stiffness,
which removes the need for strong under-relaxation. Chapter~4 validates the solver against a
compliant-tube experiment and applies it to three patient-derived aortic geometries, where it
converges in 4 to 6 sub-iterations per time step compared with 25 to 40 for a Dirichlet-Neumann
scheme.
