Legged robots can cross terrain that wheels cannot, but controlling them requires reasoning about
contacts that make and break many times per second. This thesis combines model-based and
learning-based control for quadrupedal locomotion. A reinforcement-learning policy proposes
footholds and body motions, and a model-predictive controller turns them into contact forces
that respect friction limits. Trained entirely in simulation and deployed without fine-tuning,
the controller walks over rough terrain, steps and slopes, and reduces falls on a rubble course
from 14 to 2 in 100 trials compared with a purely learned policy.
