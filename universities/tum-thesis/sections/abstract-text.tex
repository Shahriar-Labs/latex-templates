Robots that manipulate objects must predict how the objects will move when pushed, lifted or
dropped. Analytical models capture rigid contact well but struggle with friction that varies
across a surface and with objects whose mass distribution is unknown. This dissertation learns
such dynamics from interaction data while keeping the structure that physics guarantees. It
combines a differentiable contact model with a neural residual, trains the residual on 18 hours
of pushing data, and uses the model for model-predictive control. On a pushing benchmark with
unknown objects, the hybrid model cuts final position error by 41\% compared with a purely
learned model trained on the same data.
