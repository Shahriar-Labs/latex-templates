\section{Preliminaries and Notation}

We consider the unconstrained minimization problem
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}^d}\; \mathcal{L}(\theta)
    = \mathbb{E}_{\xi \sim \mathcal{D}}\bigl[\ell(\theta;\xi)\bigr],
  \label{eq:expected_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}$ is an unknown data distribution and $\ell(\cdot;\xi)$ is a differentiable,
possibly non-convex loss function. At each iteration $t$, the optimizer receives a stochastic
gradient $g_t = \nabla\ell(\theta_t; \xi_t)$ where $\xi_t \sim \mathcal{D}$ is an i.i.d.\ sample.

We make the following standing assumptions:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,style=nextline]
  \item[Assumption 1 (Smoothness).] $\mathcal{L}$ is $L$-smooth: $\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta) -
    \nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta')\| \leq L\|\theta - \theta'\|$ for all $\theta,\theta' \in
    \mathbb{R}^d$.
  \item[Assumption 2 (Unbiased gradients).] $\mathbb{E}[g_t \mid \theta_t] =
    \nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_t)$.
  \item[Assumption 3 (Bounded variance).] $\mathbb{E}[\|g_t - \nabla\mathcal{L}(\theta_t)\|^2
    \mid \theta_t] \leq \sigma^2$ for some $\sigma^2 < \infty$.
  \item[Assumption 4 (Lower bounded).] $\mathcal{L}(\theta) \geq \mathcal{L}_* > -\infty$ for
    all $\theta$.
\end{description}

These assumptions are standard in the non-convex optimization literature \cite{ghadimi2013} and
are significantly weaker than the convexity and bounded-gradient assumptions commonly invoked
in prior analyses of adaptive methods.
