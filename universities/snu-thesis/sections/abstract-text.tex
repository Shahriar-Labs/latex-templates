Predicting the properties of a molecule from its structure is central to drug discovery and
materials design, and experimental measurements are slow and costly. Graph neural networks treat
a molecule as a graph of atoms and bonds and learn representations directly from it. This
dissertation studies how to make such models accurate when labelled data are scarce. It proposes
a message-passing network that encodes bond angles and ring membership, a pre-training objective
that predicts masked substructures on ten million unlabelled molecules, and a calibration method
for its uncertainty estimates. On eight property benchmarks the pre-trained model reduces mean
absolute error by 17\% on average compared with the same architecture trained from scratch.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} graph neural networks, molecular property prediction,
self-supervised pre-training, uncertainty calibration\\
