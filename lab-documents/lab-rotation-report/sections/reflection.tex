\section{Discussion and Rotation Reflection}

\subsection{What the preliminary data do and do not show}

The imaging and analysis pipeline built during this rotation appears stable enough to
detect spontaneous microglial calcium transients repeatedly within a session (Aim 1),
and the segmentation and detection steps produce traces that are simple to inspect by
eye (Aim 2). The regional comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:bars} is the least mature part
of the project: region assignment was done manually rather than through registration
to a reference brain, and the sample sizes in Table~\ref{tab:region-counts} are too
small to support a statistical claim. The honest summary is that the tectum trend is
worth following up, not that it is established.

\subsection{Skills gained and challenges}

\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{infopanel}{Skills gained}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=0.2em]
  \item Live confocal imaging of zebrafish larvae, including mounting and
    anaesthesia dosing
  \item Writing a motion-correction and segmentation pipeline from scratch in Python
  \item Reading and interpreting raw calcium traces critically, rather than trusting
    default thresholds
  \item Keeping an imaging log detailed enough for someone else to reproduce a session
\end{itemize}
\end{infopanel}
\end{minipage}
\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{infopanel}{Challenges}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=0.2em]
  \item First two mounting attempts caused enough larval movement to make
    registration fail
  \item Threshold for transient detection required several rounds of tuning against
    visually confirmed events
  \item Manual region assignment is slow and will not scale past a handful of larvae
  \item Balancing pipeline development against actually collecting data, given the
    ten-week rotation length
\end{itemize}
\end{infopanel}
\end{minipage}
\par

\subsection{Fit with future plans}

This rotation confirmed that I enjoy the combination of wet-lab imaging and the
analysis work needed to make sense of it, and that I would rather build a small
pipeline myself than rely entirely on an existing one, even when that costs time
early on. If I were to continue in the Nimbus Neurobiology Lab, the natural next step
would be to replace manual region assignment with registration to a published larval
zebrafish brain atlas, which would make the regional comparison in
Section~3.2 defensible rather than merely suggestive. I discussed this
possibility with \reportsupervisor\ at the final check-in, and a short summary of that
conversation appears in the supervisor's comments below.
