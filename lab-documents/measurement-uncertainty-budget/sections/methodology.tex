\section{Measurement Model}

The conventional mass correction of the test item, $\delta m_X$, is obtained
from the mean comparator reading difference $\Delta_m$ between the test item
and the reference standard, corrected for the reference standard's own known
correction $\delta m_S$, drift since its last calibration $\delta m_D$, air
buoyancy $\delta m_B$, and residual instrument effects (resolution,
eccentricity, thermal, and magnetic interaction), each treated as an additive
correction with an associated sensitivity coefficient.

\begin{modelbox}
\begin{equation}
  \delta m_X \;=\; \Delta_m \;+\; \delta m_S \;+\; \delta m_D \;+\; \delta m_B
  \;+\; \delta m_R \;+\; \delta m_E \;+\; \delta m_T \;+\; \delta m_M
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-4pt}
\noindent{\small where $\Delta_m$ is the mean of $n$ ABBA comparison cycles
(Type~A) and every other term on the right-hand side is a Type~B correction
drawn from calibration certificates, manufacturer specification, or
engineering judgement.}
\end{modelbox}

\subsection{Type A Evaluation}
The repeatability component is the experimental standard deviation of the
mean of $n = 10$ ABBA comparison cycles:
\begin{equation}
  u_A = \frac{s(\Delta_m)}{\sqrt{n}}, \qquad
  s(\Delta_m) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\Delta_{m,k} - \overline{\Delta_m}\right)^2}
\end{equation}
with $n - 1 = 9$ degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Type B Evaluation}
Each Type~B input $x_i$ is characterised by an assumed probability
distribution (normal, from a calibration certificate stating an expanded
uncertainty $U$ and coverage factor $k$; or rectangular, for a bound
$a$ with no further information on the value's location) and converted to a
standard uncertainty $u(x_i) = U/k$ or $u(x_i) = a/\sqrt{3}$, respectively.

\subsection{Propagation}
For each input quantity $x_i$ the contribution to the combined standard
uncertainty of the measurand $y = f(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ is
\begin{equation}
  u_i(y) = \ci \, u(x_i), \qquad
  \ci = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}
\end{equation}
Because the model in Eq.~\eqref{eq:model} is purely additive in mass units,
every sensitivity coefficient in this budget is $\ci = 1$ or $\ci = -1$
(dimensionless), so each $u_i(y)$ is numerically equal to $u(x_i)$.
