\section{Sample Preparation}

\subsection{Cell or Tissue Lysis}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cell culture:} Aspirate medium. Wash cells twice with ice-cold PBS.
        Place plate on ice. Add \vol{200--500} NexaLysis\texttrademark~RIPA Buffer
        per 60\,mm dish (scale proportionally).
  \item Add $1\times$ protease inhibitor cocktail immediately before use. Optional:
        add phosphatase inhibitors (Cat.\ NB-INH-007) if phospho-proteins are targets.
  \item Scrape cells into a 1.5\,mL microcentrifuge tube. Incubate on ice for
        \mintime{30} with occasional vortexing every \mintime{5}.
  \item \textbf{Tissue:} Homogenise 10--30\,mg tissue in \vol{300} lysis buffer
        using a pre-chilled pestle. Proceed identically to step~3.
  \item Centrifuge at $16\,000 \times g$, \degC{4}, \mintime{15}.
        Carefully transfer the supernatant (whole-cell lysate) to a fresh tube.
        Discard pellet.
  \item Snap-freeze aliquots in liquid nitrogen or dry ice/ethanol slurry.
        Store at \degC{-80}. Avoid more than three freeze-thaw cycles.
\end{enumerate}

\par
\begin{tipbox}{Best Practice — Nuclear Fractions}
If nuclear or membrane proteins are targets, use a fractionation kit
(NB-FRAC-002) rather than bulk RIPA lysis, which may under-extract these
compartments.
\end{tipbox}

\subsection{Protein Quantification}

\par\vspace{4pt}
Use the BCA or Bradford assay according to kit instructions. Recommended range:
\conc{0.5--5}{\ensuremath{\mu} g/\ensuremath{\mu} L}. Measure absorbance at 562\,nm (BCA) or
595\,nm (Bradford). Ensure standards are prepared in the same lysis buffer as
samples to minimise matrix effects.

\subsection{Sample Denaturation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Dilute samples to a uniform protein concentration in lysis buffer.
        Equalise volumes across all lanes.
  \item Add Laemmli sample buffer ($4\times$) at a ratio of 1\,:\,3
        (buffer\,:\,sample) to reach $1\times$ final concentration.
  \item Heat at \degC{95} for \mintime{5} (or \degC{70} for \mintime{10}
        for membrane proteins such as multi-pass transmembrane receptors).
  \item Briefly centrifuge at $800 \times g$ for \mintime{1} to collect condensate.
  \item Samples are ready for immediate loading or may be stored at \degC{-20}
        for up to two weeks.
\end{enumerate}

\par
\begin{notebox}{DTT vs.\ \ensuremath{\beta}-Mercaptoethanol}
Reducing agent choice affects downstream assays. DTT (\conc{100}{mM} final) is
odourless and preferred for ECL. \ensuremath{\beta}-Mercaptoethanol (\conc{5}{\%}) provides
stronger reduction for stubborn disulfide bridges but must be handled in a fume hood.
\end{notebox}
