\subsection{Measured Stratigraphic Section}

(Unit~I) to the uppermost preserved horizon (Unit~VII).

\subsection{Lithology Description Table}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{} l l >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Unit} & \textbf{Age (approx.)} & \textbf{Lithology \& Key Features} \\
\midrule
I   & Miocene (11--10\,Ma)  & White to cream massive diatomite; chalky texture; rare mollusc fragments indicating fresh-water lacustrine origin; sub-horizontal lamination. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
II  & Miocene (10--9\,Ma)   & Pale yellow tuffaceous siltstone; abundant glass shards (optical microscopy); occasional plant-leaf impressions; even parallel bedding. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
III & Miocene (9.2\,Ma)    & White-grey compact volcanic ash (airfall tuff); sharp basal contact; pumice clasts up to 2\,cm; K-feldspar phenocrysts visible; potential radiometric marker bed. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
IV  & Miocene (9--7.5\,Ma)  & Red to brick-orange arkosic sandstone; medium--coarse grain; sub-angular to sub-rounded; cross-bedding sets 20--40\,cm; K-feldspar and quartz dominant; plagioclase partially altered to sericite. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
V   & Miocene (7.5--7\,Ma)  & Light grey to buff micrite and wackestone; discrete algal laminae; vugs with calcite spar; minor dolomitisation near top; represents return to lacustrine conditions. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
VI  & Pliocene (5--3\,Ma)   & Yellow-brown pebbly sandstone; clasts 0.5--3\,cm subangular quartzite and granite; fining-upward cycles 1--2\,m thick; fluvial channel facies. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
VII & Pleistocene (2--1\,Ma) & Clast-supported polymict conglomerate; cobbles to 20\,cm; poorly sorted; sub-angular clasts of granite, gneiss, quartzite; sandy matrix; alluvial fan interpretation. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
