\subsection*{Data Management Policy}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em]
  \item \textbf{Storage.} Raw data lives on Nimbus Drive under
        \texttt{/projects/\textless project\textgreater/raw/}; analysis code and derived
        data live in the matching \texttt{/derived/} folder. Nothing important lives only on
        a laptop.
  \item \textbf{Backup.} Nimbus Drive is backed up nightly and versioned for 90 days.
        Electrophysiology rigs additionally write a same-day copy to the rig PC's local disk,
        cleared once Nimbus confirms sync.
  \item \textbf{Naming.} Files follow \texttt{YYYYMMDD\_subjectID\_condition\_v\#}. Consistency
        here is what makes a dataset usable by someone other than you in two years.
  \item \textbf{Retention.} Raw data and lab notebooks are retained for a minimum of 7 years
        past publication, per university policy and our funders' requirements.
  \item \textbf{Access \& sharing.} Data is shared within the lab by default; sharing outside
        the lab (collaborators, repositories, reviewers) requires your mentor's sign-off,
        logged as a note in the project's Vertex ELN page.
  \item \textbf{De-identification.} Any human-subjects data (surveys, pilot studies) is
        de-identified at the point of entry --- no names or identifiers in filenames, folder
        names, or code.
  \item \textbf{Public release.} On publication, processed data and analysis code are
        deposited to a public repository under an open licence, consistent with our funders'
        data-sharing plans (see Further Reading).
\end{itemize}

\begin{factbox}{Code Conventions}
Analysis code lives in the lab's shared repository, one folder per project, with a short
\texttt{README} describing what each script produces. Commit messages describe \emph{why}, not
just \emph{what} --- the diff already shows what changed.
\end{factbox}
