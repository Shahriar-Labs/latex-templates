\subsection{Exposure Details}
\begin{itemize}[label=\textcolor{alertred}{\faExclamationTriangle}]
    \item \textbf{Type of Exposure:} Chemical splash leading to direct skin contact.
    \item \textbf{Anatomical Location:} Right lateral neck (small splash) and dorsal aspect of the right wrist/forearm (approx. 3\,cm area of contact).
    \item \textbf{Inhalation or Ingestion:} None reported. Eyewash was used as a precaution, but safety glasses successfully prevented ocular exposure.
    \item \textbf{Physical Symptoms:} Immediate burning sensation, followed by localized erythema (redness) and mild swelling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Medical Response \& First Aid Treatment}
Following the splash, the following response protocol was executed immediately:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Immediate Wash:} Marcus Vance assisted Dr. Jenkins to the emergency safety shower. The affected neck and arm areas were flushed with cold water for a continuous 15 minutes.
    \item \textbf{Contaminated Clothing Removal:} Dr. Jenkins removed her contaminated lab coat and outer shirt while in the shower area to prevent further absorption.
    \item \textbf{Emergency Transport:} Dr. Robert Chen (Lab Manager) escorted Dr. Jenkins to the Vertex Occupational Health Center (Building C).
    \item \textbf{Clinical Care:} Occupational Health Physician Dr. Alan Turing examined the affected areas. He diagnosed first-degree chemical burns from acetyl chloride hydrolysis (releasing hydrochloric acid). The areas were cleaned, treated with a topical soothing ointment, and bandaged.
    \item \textbf{Prognosis:} Dr. Jenkins was cleared to return to light duties with no lost time. A follow-up check was scheduled for 48 hours post-incident.
\end{enumerate}
