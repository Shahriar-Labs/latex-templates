\section{Immediate Actions and Spill Containment}
\thinrule

\vspace{0.5em}

This section documents the immediate actions taken to control the hazard, secure the area, neutralize the chemical spill, and ensure the safety of other lab personnel.

\subsection{Emergency Containment \& Spill Response}
Directly after securing the safety of the exposed personnel, the remaining lab staff acted to contain the hazard:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Power Shutdown:} Marcus Vance immediately switched off the power source to the heating mantle and magnetic stirrer for Hood \#12 to prevent further temperature rises or thermal runaway.
    \item \textbf{Spill Neutralization:} Since the splash involved acetyl chloride (which reacts violently with water to release hydrochloric acid gas), using water for spill cleanup was strictly avoided. Vance used the spill kit located in Lab 304B to apply an amphoteric neutralizing absorbent (brand: Spill-Sorb) over the spilled liquid inside the fume hood.
    \item \textbf{Ventilation Boost:} The fume hood exhaust was set to "Emergency Purge" mode to maximize air flow and draw out any hydrochloric acid (HCl) vapors generated by hydrolysis of the acetyl chloride with ambient humidity.
    \item \textbf{Area Isolation:} The laboratory room 304B was evacuated of all other research students and staff. A temporary warning sign reading \textbf{"DO NOT ENTER -- Chemical Spill Containment in Progress"} was posted on the main door.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Waste Disposal and Site Remediation}
The neutralized spill residue and contaminated absorbent were collected in a heavy-duty polypropylene container, labeled as hazardous chemical waste, and transferred to the Vertex hazardous waste storage bunker. The volume of waste collected was approximately 1.5\,L of solid-absorbent mixture. Fume Hood \#12 was locked and tagged out of service (\textbf{LOTO ID: LOTO-2026-0803}) pending a full inspection of the ventilation sashes and mechanical exhaust system.
