\section{Air Flow and Air Distribution System Design}
\label{sec:air_distribution}

The supply airflow rate required to satisfy the sensible cooling loads is calculated using:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\text{supply}} = \frac{q_{\text{sens}}}{1.1 \cdot \rho_{\text{std}} \cdot (T_{\text{room}} - T_{\text{supply}})}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\text{supply}}$ is the airflow rate in CFM, $q_{\text{sens}}$ is the sensible cooling load in Btu/h, $\rho_{\text{std}}$ is the air density correction factor, $T_{\text{room}}$ is the room design dry-bulb temperature (75$^{\circ}$F), and $T_{\text{supply}}$ is the design supply air temperature off the cooling coil (55$^{\circ}$F).

For the total sensible peak load of 591.5 MBH (excluding ventilation load which is handled directly by the pre-conditioner), the required zone supply airflow is:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\text{supply}} = \frac{591,500}{1.1 \cdot (75 - 55)} \approx 26,886 \text{ CFM}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ductwork Design and Sizing}
Duct design was performed using the equal friction method, setting a design pressure drop of 0.1 in. w.g. per 100 ft (0.82 Pa/m) for supply ducts, and 0.08 in. w.g. per 100 ft (0.65 Pa/m) for return ducts. Velocities in main ducts were capped at 1,500 FPM (7.6 m/s) to control noise and vibration levels, in compliance with ASHRAE Handbook guidelines for commercial office buildings.

\subsection{External Static Pressure (ESP) Calculation}
A detailed pressure loss estimation was carried out for the index run (longest run) to size the supply fan motor. The results are summarized below:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Straight duct friction losses: 0.35 in. w.g.
  \item Duct fittings and transitions (tees, elbows, dampers): 0.28 in. w.g.
  \item Terminal units (VAV boxes with reheat coils): 0.45 in. w.g.
  \item Diffusers, grilles, and registers: 0.15 in. w.g.
  \item Air Handling Unit internal losses (filters, coils, silencers): 0.85 in. w.g.
  \item \textbf{Total Static Pressure Required:} \textbf{2.08 in. w.g.} (including 15\% safety margin).
\end{itemize}
