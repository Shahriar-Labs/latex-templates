\section{Conclusion and Recommendations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The detailed HVAC design and thermal load calculations for the Synapse Headquarters office complex demonstrate a robust engineering approach tailored to the demanding hot-humid climate of Austin, Texas. By employing high-performance envelope materials exceeding ASHRAE 90.1 standard limits, peak cooling and heating thermal loads were kept within manageable bounds:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Peak Cooling Load: 650.7 MBH sensible/latent (54.2 Tons capacity).
  \item Peak Heating Load: 320.4 MBH sensible.
  \item Central Supply Airflow: 22,000 CFM based on central VAV zoning.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Energy Efficiency Enhancements}
While the current design baseline represents a highly efficient VAV system, the following engineering enhancements are recommended during the detailed design phase to further improve sustainability and reduce operational costs:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Energy Recovery Ventilation (ERV):} Implementing an air-to-air energy recovery wheel within the pre-conditioner unit. This would pre-cool and dehumidify the warm, humid outdoor air in summer using the cool, dry exhaust air, reducing peak ventilation load by up to 22\%.
  \item \textbf{Demand Controlled Ventilation (DCV):} Installing carbon dioxide (CO$_{2}$) sensors in high-occupancy zones (conference rooms, cafeteria) to modulate outdoor air dampers based on real-time occupancy, avoiding unnecessary conditioning of ventilation air during partial occupancy.
  \item \textbf{Chilled Water Reset Strategy:} Implementing a controls algorithm that raises the chilled water supply temperature during low cooling load periods, increasing the chiller COP by 1.5\% per degree Fahrenheit of reset.
\end{enumerate}

By incorporating these strategies, the mechanical systems will not only provide excellent thermal comfort but will also position the Synapse Headquarters as a leader in sustainable facility design.
