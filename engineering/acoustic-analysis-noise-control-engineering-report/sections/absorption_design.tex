\section{Sound Absorption \& Reverberation Control Design}
The main testing hall of the Synapse Laboratory Complex features high ceilings ($6.0\text{~m}$) and hard, reflective concrete surfaces. This results in excessive reverberation, which degrades speech intelligibility and presents safety hazards due to localized echo flutter.

\subsection{Sabine Formula and Reverberation Modeling}
Reverberation time ($T_{60}$) calculations were performed using Sabine's classical formula:
\begin{equation}
    T_{60} = 0.161 \frac{V}{\sum S_i \alpha_i}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the room volume ($2400\text{~m}^3$), $S_i$ is the surface area of each boundary element, and $\alpha_i$ is the absorption coefficient of that element at a given frequency.

\subsection{Acoustic Panels and Material Specifications}
To achieve the target reverberation time of $T_{60} \le 1.2\text{~seconds}$ for safety and utility, we specify the installation of Apex SoundSorb suspended baffle panels. The absorption coefficients of these panels compared to concrete are detailed in Table~\ref{tab:absorption_coefficients}.

\subsection{Reverberation Profile Reduction}
The planned installation of $120\text{~m}^2$ of Apex SoundSorb panels on the ceiling and upper walls increases the total equivalent absorption area of the hall from $34.2\text{~m}^2\text{--Sabin}$ to $146.5\text{~m}^2\text{--Sabin}$ at $500\text{~Hz}$. This reduces the calculated reverberation time at $500\text{~Hz}$ from $5.2\text{~seconds}$ (unacceptable) to $1.1\text{~seconds}$, which is well within our safety and engineering design target~\cite{Kuttruff2009}.
