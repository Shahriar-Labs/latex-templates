\section{Sound Transmission Loss Analysis}\label{sec:mitigation}
To isolate high-amplitude low-frequency noise generated in the mechanical engine room, high-performance acoustic partitions are required. The wall separating the mechanical room from the precision spectroscopy lab must achieve a Sound Transmission Class (STC) rating of at least $55$ to prevent disruption to delicate laser diagnostic experiments.

\subsection{Partition Assemblies}
We analyzed two partition assemblies using ASTM E90 test procedures and transmission loss modeling:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Baseline Assembly (STC 38):} Standard double-stud partition consisting of a single layer of $12.7\text{~mm}$ standard gypsum board on each side of $90\text{~mm}$ steel studs spaced $600\text{~mm}$ on center, with no cavity insulation.
    \item \textbf{Optimized Assembly (STC 56):} Double-stud partition with two layers of $15.9\text{~mm}$ type-X gypsum board on each side, mounted on resilient channels (RC-1) on the lab side, with the stud cavity completely filled with $75\text{~mm}$ mineral wool batts ($60\text{~kg/m}^3$).
\end{enumerate}

The resilient mounting and high-density mineral wool cavity fill resolve the coincident frequency dip of the gypsum board around $2.5\text{~kHz}$ and significantly improve low-frequency performance below $250\text{~Hz}$, achieving the target STC 56 threshold~\cite{Bies2009}.
