\section{Pressure Relief \& Flare System Sizing}
\label{sec:relief_sizing}

The emergency relief system for reactor R-101 is sized using the DIERS methodology for runaway chemical reactions, in compliance with API Standards 520 and 521~\cite{api520, api521}. Due to liquid swell, two-phase (gas-liquid) flow is expected during venting.

\subsection{Design Basis and Sizing Methodology}
The controlling scenario is a \textbf{loss of cooling water leading to runaway reaction}. This scenario produces the highest required mass venting rate due to solvent vaporization and autocatalytic product decomposition.

The required relief area ($A$) is calculated using the Homogeneous Equilibrium Model (HEM) for choked flow:
\begin{equation}
A = \frac{W_v}{K_d \cdot G_c}
\end{equation}
where $W_v$ is the peak venting rate ($42,500 \text{ kg/h}$), $K_d$ is the discharge coefficient ($0.85$), and $G_c$ is the critical two-phase mass flux ($3,250 \text{ kg/m}^2\cdot\text{s}$) calculated by integrating the momentum equation:
\begin{equation}
G_c = \rho_t \sqrt{2 (h_0 - h_t)}
\end{equation}
Here, $\rho_t$ and $h_t$ represent fluid properties at the throat, and $h_0$ is the stagnation enthalpy.

\subsection{Piping Pressure Drop Constraints}
Per API 520 Part II, the inlet piping pressure drop must not exceed $3\%$ of the set pressure to prevent valve chatter. The $3$-inch inlet line layout limits the friction loss to $0.21 \text{ bar}$ ($2.1\%$). The backpressure on the outlet line is limited by using a balanced bellows PSV, which tolerates backpressures up to $50\%$. The $4$-inch discharge line to the flare header has a calculated backpressure of $3.5 \text{ barg}$ ($35\%$), which satisfies all design constraints.
