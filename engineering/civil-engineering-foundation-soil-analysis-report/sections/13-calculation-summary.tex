\section{Calculation summary}
\subsection{Pile resistance arithmetic}
For $D=\SI{0.60}{\metre}$, shaft perimeter $p=\pi D=\SI{1.885}{\metre}$ and base area
$A_b=\pi D^2/4=\SI{0.2827}{\metre\squared}$. Applying \cref{eq:pile}:
\begin{align*}
Q_{s,\mathrm{clay}} &= 1.885(2.5)(22) = \SI{104}{\kilo\newton},\\
Q_{s,\mathrm{med\ sand}} &= 1.885(6.0)(45) = \SI{509}{\kilo\newton},\\
Q_{s,\mathrm{dense\ sand}} &= 1.885(7.0)(80) = \SI{1056}{\kilo\newton},\\
Q_b &= 0.2827(3500) = \SI{990}{\kilo\newton},\\
Q_u &= 104+509+1056+990 = \SI{2659}{\kilo\newton},\\
Q_{\mathrm{allow,calc}} &= 2659/2.5 = \SI{1064}{\kilo\newton},\\
Q_{\mathrm{allow,adopted}} &= \SI{900}{\kilo\newton}.
\end{align*}
The calculation omits shaft resistance in uncontrolled fill and does not count pile self-weight
as resistance. Final partial factors, load combinations and downdrag treatment shall follow the
governing code.

\subsection{Example pile count}
For a service column reaction $N_s=\SI{1800}{\kilo\newton}$, the arithmetic minimum is
\begin{equation*}
n=\left\lceil\frac{N_s}{Q_{\mathrm{allow}}}\right\rceil
=\left\lceil\frac{1800}{900}\right\rceil=2.
\end{equation*}
A two-pile cap may be unsuitable where moments act in both directions; the structural designer
may require three or four piles to achieve stability and acceptable load distribution. Pile count
shall be based on the complete service and ultimate action envelopes, including cap self-weight,
uplift, eccentricity and seismic combinations.
