\section{Groundwater, excavation and seismic considerations}
\subsection{Basement excavation and water control}
The assumed excavation base is approximately \SI{0.5}{\metre} below the observed water level.
Open pumping without filters can cause piping, loss of fines and settlement outside the site.
The contractor's temporary-works design should include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a cut-off or internally filtered wellpoint / sump arrangement matched to the sand layers;
  \item piezometers inside and outside the excavation, with trigger levels tied to response actions;
  \item stability checks for basal heave, piping, uplift and wall toe embedment;
  \item recharge or staged pumping where drawdown at adjacent foundations is unacceptable; and
  \item discharge permits, treatment and a standby power/pump plan.
\end{itemize}

Permanent basement design shall assume hydrostatic pressure to the project design groundwater
level unless a maintainable permanent drainage system is deliberately adopted. Buoyancy should
be checked for both completed and temporary construction stages.

\subsection{Liquefaction and cyclic response}
The medium-dense silty sand below the water table is potentially susceptible to cyclic pore-pressure
generation. A definitive liquefaction assessment cannot be made from the assumed data because
design earthquake demand, fines plasticity and reliable energy-corrected SPT/CPT data are absent.
Obtain seismic CPT and preferably shear-wave velocity measurements; evaluate triggering,
post-liquefaction settlement, lateral deformation and pile kinematic demand using the governing
hazard level. The preliminary soil profile should be treated conservatively until that assessment
is complete.
