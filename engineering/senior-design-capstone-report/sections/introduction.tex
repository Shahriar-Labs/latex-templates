\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Project Background}
Last-mile delivery on dense university campuses is constrained by narrow sidewalks,
mixed pedestrian traffic, and short trip distances that make conventional wheeled
courier services inefficient. \ProjectSponsor\ approached the \ProjectDept\ with a
challenge: design a small autonomous ground vehicle capable of carrying food and
small packages across the Meridian campus without a dedicated bike-lane or road
right-of-way, while remaining safe around pedestrians and compliant with the campus
mobility code (Ordinance 14.3, sidewalk-legal robot classification).

\ProjectTeamName\ was formed to answer this challenge over two semesters
(ENGR~4901 in the fall, ENGR~4902 in the spring). This report documents the
problem definition, the engineering requirements derived from stakeholder input,
the design alternatives considered, the final embodiment that was built and
tested, and the verification results collected during the spring test campaign.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
Design, build, and validate an autonomous delivery robot that can navigate
approximately 1.2\,km of mixed campus pathway, carry a 6\,kg payload at a
controlled temperature, operate for a full academic day on a single charge,
and detect and yield to pedestrians without operator intervention.

\subsection{Project Objectives}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Achieve a mean navigation success rate $\geq 95\%$ across three
        representative campus routes.
  \item Keep unit bill-of-materials cost under \$2{,}200 to support a
        fleet-scale business case for \ProjectSponsor.
  \item Demonstrate a minimum obstacle-to-stop response time of 300\,ms
        at the robot's cruising speed.
  \item Deliver a design that a two-person team can service (battery swap,
        payload-bay cleaning) in under 5 minutes.
\end{itemize}
