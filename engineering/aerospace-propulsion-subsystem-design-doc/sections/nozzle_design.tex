\section{Nozzle Contour \& Aerodynamic Design}
The expansion of the combustion products from the throat to the exit plane is designed using the Method of Characteristics (MOC) to produce a shock-free, optimized parabolic contour (Rao Bell nozzle)~\cite{Anderson2003}. This minimizes divergence losses while keeping nozzle length and mass within stage limits.

\subsection{Geometric Profile}
The expansion ratio $\epsilon = A_e / A_t = 80.0$ yields a high area expansion suited for vacuum operation. The exit diameter $D_e$ is calculated as:
\begin{equation}
    D_e = D_t \sqrt{\epsilon} = 158.9 \text{~mm} \cdot \sqrt{80} = 1421.2 \text{~mm} = 1.421 \text{~m}
\end{equation}

The nozzle convergent section uses a standard $60^\circ$ half-angle convergence, and the divergent section starts at the throat with an initial expansion angle $\theta_i = 18.2^\circ$ and terminates at the exit plane with a terminal angle $\theta_e = 8.5^\circ$. The overall length of the nozzle divergent section is $1.85\text{~m}$, representing an $80\%$ bell nozzle.

\subsection{Mach Number Profile}
As the combustion products expand through the convergent-divergent nozzle, the flow transitions from subsonic ($M < 1$) in the chamber to sonic ($M = 1$) at the throat, and supersonic ($M > 1$) in the divergent section. 
The relationship between Mach number $M$ and area ratio $A/A_t$ is determined by the 1D isentropic relation:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{A}{A_t} = \frac{1}{M} \left[ \frac{2}{\gamma+1} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2} M^2 \right) \right]^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}
\end{equation}
For $\gamma = 1.22$ and exit area expansion $\epsilon = 80.0$, this yields an exit Mach number of:
\begin{equation}
    M_e = 4.62
\end{equation}
resulting in high kinetic energy conversion and directed momentum.
