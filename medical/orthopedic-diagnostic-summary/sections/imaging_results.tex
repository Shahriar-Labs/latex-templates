\section{Diagnostic Imaging Analysis}
\label{sec:imaging}

\subsection{Plain Radiographs (Right Knee - 3 Views)}
Anteroposterior (AP), lateral, and sunrise views of the right knee obtained on June 12, 2026, reveal:
\begin{itemize}
  \item No acute fractures or dislocations.
  \item Normal joint space alignment with no significant osteoarthritic changes.
  \item A small, avulsed bony fragment off the lateral tibial plateau, consistent with a \textbf{Segond fracture} (pathognomonic for ACL injury) \cite{meridian2025segond}.
  \item Mild soft-tissue swelling and joint effusion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI - Right Knee)}
A high-resolution, non-contrast MRI of the right knee was performed on June 28, 2026. The findings confirm multi-ligamentous and meniscal pathology:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL):} There is a complete, mid-substance tear of the ACL with non-visualization of the normal low-signal fiber bundle. The remaining proximal stub is retracted and horizontalized. There is associated bone bruising (microtrabecular fractures) in the middle of the lateral femoral condyle and the posterior aspect of the lateral tibial plateau, representing a classic "kissing contusion" pattern.
  \item \textbf{Lateral Meniscus:} There is a longitudinal, vertical tear in the posterior horn of the lateral meniscus, extending to the articular surface. The tear measures approximately 1.2~cm in length but remains stable with no displacement into the joint space (no bucket-handle configuration).
  \item \textbf{Medial Meniscus:} Intact. No signal abnormality or tear noted.
  \item \textbf{Collateral Ligaments (MCL/LCL) and Patellar Tendon:} The medial and lateral collateral ligaments are intact with normal signal intensity. The patellar and quadriceps tendons are unremarkable.
\end{enumerate}
