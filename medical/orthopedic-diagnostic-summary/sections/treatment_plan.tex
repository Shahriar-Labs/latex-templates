\section{Diagnostic Impression}
\label{sec:impression}

Based on clinical history, physical examination, and magnetic resonance imaging, the patient's diagnoses are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Acute Complete Rupture of the Right Anterior Cruciate Ligament (ACL):} ICD-10 Code: \textbf{S83.511A} (Disruption of anterior cruciate ligament of right knee, initial encounter).
  \item \textbf{Longitudinal Vertical Tear of the Posterior Horn of the Lateral Meniscus:} ICD-10 Code: \textbf{S83.241A} (Other tear of lateral meniscus, right knee, initial encounter).
  \item \textbf{Segond Fracture of the Right Lateral Tibial Plateau:} ICD-10 Code: \textbf{S82.101A} (Unspecified fracture of upper end of right tibia, initial encounter).
  \item \textbf{Secondary Post-Traumatic Joint Effusion:} ICD-10 Code: \textbf{M25.461} (Effusion, right knee).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Treatment Recommendations}
\label{sec:recommendations}

\begin{recommendationbox}{Primary Surgical Plan}
  \textbf{Procedure:} Right Knee Arthroscopic Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction with Autograft (Quadriceps Tendon) and Arthroscopic Lateral Meniscus Repair. \\
  \textbf{Facility:} Meridian Surgical Center, Operating Room 4. \\
  \textbf{Implants/Equipment:} Vertex Orthopedics Bioscrew fixation, Synapse Medical HD Arthroscopy Platform.
\end{recommendationbox}

Given the patient's age, high physical demands (runner), and symptomatic instability, non-operative management is highly likely to result in progressive joint degeneration and recurrent injury. Reconstruction is recommended to restore functional stability and protect the joint from long-term osteoarthritis \cite{shea2022acl}.

\subsection{Surgical Technique Details}
The procedure will be performed under general anesthesia combined with a femoral nerve block for postoperative analgesia. A standard diagnostic arthroscopy will be completed to inspect all compartments. The lateral meniscus tear will be repaired using an inside-out or all-inside suture technique (utilizing 2--3 suture anchors) to ensure stable reduction \cite{vance2024meniscal}.

The ACL reconstruction will be performed using a quadriceps tendon autograft harvested from the ipsilateral knee. Femoral and tibial tunnels will be drilled academically. The graft will be secured under tension using a suspensory cortical button on the femur and a bioabsorbable interference screw (Vertex Orthopedics) on the tibia.

\subsection{Surgical Risks and Consent}
The risks of the procedure, including infection, graft failure, persistent stiffness (arthrofibrosis), deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and the potential need for secondary procedures, were discussed in detail with the patient. The patient has verbalized understanding of these risks and wishes to proceed with the surgical plan.
